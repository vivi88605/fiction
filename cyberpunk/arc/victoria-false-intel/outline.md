# The False Intel — Plot Beats

## Setup

**When:** Victoria is 18, a few months into active service — fresh out of the academy, still carrying its training intact.

**Squad:** Garnet Four, a line squad before Turquoise Nine. She's its newest member and junior marksman/technician.

**The intel:** The sector has been marked _stabilized_ ahead of a campaign milestone. Days earlier, a forward drone logged signs of a hostile indirect-fire capability still active in the area. Through consolidation the report goes _unconfirmed_ → _low confidence_ → _residual_ → absent from the squad-level brief. Every step is defensible. Nobody lies. The overwatch position is chosen for sightlines over cover, because the brief says cover isn't the concern.

**Core thread:** Victoria begins the arc trusting the system completely — not naively, but the way she was trained to: intel is verified upstream, that's what upstream is for. She ends it as the person who trusts nothing she hasn't checked herself. The turn isn't a betrayal. It's the discovery that nobody betrayed her at all.

**The squad stays a blur, deliberately:** She's been with them only a few months and hasn't let anyone in. She knows them mostly as callsigns and voices on comms. She hadn't started bracing for any of their deaths yet — which, afterward, feels like its own failure.

**POV:** Victoria throughout.

**Tone:** Visceral but not graphic — the injury lives in sensation, disorientation, sound, and absence, never in description of damage.

---

## The Op — Trust Intact

- Routine overwatch. Quiet sector, per the brief.
- Victoria is careful, disciplined, already herself — but her caution works _inside_ the system, not against it. She checks her own gear obsessively; she doesn't check the brief, because checking the brief isn't her job.
- Small texture of the squad: callsigns, a joke on comms she doesn't laugh at, someone offering her something she declines. None of it lands as a person yet.
- She's prone behind a low parapet — the position was picked for sightlines, not cover. Her lower face is below the cover line; her eyes are above it, at the scope.
- Her world is the scope: magnified, precise, controlled. The last thing she sees with her own eyes is through glass.

---

## The Hit

- No warning she'd recognize. Pressure before sound. White.
- The parapet takes what's below the line. Everything above it is gone. (Never described directly — it's understood from what she can and can't feel, and later from what the doctors tell her.)
- Then sensation in fragments:
  - heat down one side
  - ringing that swallows everything
  - dust and metal in her mouth
  - she opens her eyes, or thinks she does, and there's nothing
- Her hands find her rifle by habit.
- The vitals monitor is alarming inside her head — the corp's system knows she's hurt before she does.
- Comms: callsigns overlapping. One of them doesn't answer. It keeps not answering.
- Her trained reflex is to report. She tries to give a sitrep and can't give position, direction, count — anything. Information is the one thing she's supposed to produce, and she can't.
- Casevac in pieces: being moved, a hand on her shoulder, a voice telling her not to talk.
- She asks about the squad. Nobody answers her.
- Dark.

---

## The Dark — Blind Recovery

- She wakes in a medical facility. Bandages. The darkness is total and doesn't change.
- She's told clinically: both eyes gone, upper face destroyed, skull reinforcement held. Her mouth and jaw are intact — she can still talk, which is how she keeps herself oriented. Reconstruction and optic implants scheduled.
- Her head has been shaved for surgery. She finds out by touch, not by being told.
- She keeps herself functioning the only way she knows — methodically:
  - maps the room by sound
  - counts steps to the door, the bathroom
  - learns staff by footsteps and voices
  - keeps her own sense of time when no one tells her the hour
- Casualties reach her as numbers: one killed, others wounded. She tries to match the dead callsign to a face and can't quite do it. She never will now.
- Automated duty-of-care messages arrive in a synthetic voice: wellness notices, a commendation, _no one gets left behind._ She believes it. It's comforting, in the dark, to be accounted for.
- No one from the squad visits. They've redeployed. Maybe one short message.

### Reconstruction

- Reconstruction starts within the first couple of weeks, while she's still blind — the bone framework has to go in before swelling and scarring set in.
- Concord has no scan of her original face on file. Rebuilding from a biometric template is standard practice in cases like hers.
- The contract goes to the lowest-bid provider, who picks a stock template that's close enough rather than doing anything custom.
- She can't read the consent paperwork. A synthetic voice reads her a summary, and she authorizes by voice.
- She doesn't expect to look the same afterward. She's told the result will be "a slightly different face," and she decides she's fine with that. It's a face; being functional matters more. The decision feels sensible.
- Surgeries in the dark, felt rather than seen: pressure, dressings, swelling, the tightness of new tissue. She can touch her own mouth and jaw, which still feel like hers. Above them there's only bandage.
- Post-op report: _within acceptable parameters._

### The Official Account

- A liaison delivers the after-action summary: _enemy adaptation, unforeseeable, sector status unchanged._
- Her billet has been filled "to preserve operational continuity."
- She accepts all of it. A small dissonance registers — _sector status unchanged_ — and she files it away without looking at it.

---

## Eyes Online

- Weeks later, once the sockets have healed: optic implant surgery.
- The first thing she sees isn't the world. It's a boot screen, a calibration grid, diagnostic text.
- Then the world arrives — processed, overlaid, range-tagged. It's clearer than her old eyes ever were.
- Quiet realization: everything she sees now is data a system hands her. Her own sight has become a feed.
- Calibration sessions. She's good at it and asks the tech too many questions. It's the first time she's wanted to know how the thing that tells her the truth actually works.
- Her face is still dressed and swollen. She could look at any time now and doesn't. She tells herself there's no point until the swelling goes down — a reasonable excuse, which is what makes it easy to keep using.

---

## The Face

- Over the following weeks the swelling subsides and the dressings come off in stages. The face surfaces gradually, becoming someone's a little more each day.
- Once it's settled enough to be _a face_, she finally looks properly. She goes in expecting to be fine; she already agreed to this.
- She isn't fine. What she decided she could accept in the abstract, she can't accept looking back at her. Her own assessment of herself was wrong, and it was her word — _acceptable_ — that let it happen.
- A seam, not a stranger. Her own mouth and jaw, and above them someone else's eyes, brows, nose bridge — blended so smoothly the join is almost invisible. Not scarred, not ugly — that's what's wrong. Plausible.
- That's worse than a full stranger would have been: she can't disown it completely. When she speaks, it's her mouth moving under someone else's eyes.
- The eyes are blue. Hers had been a little greener. It's subtle enough that no one else would ever notice, and it's the detail she can't stop seeing.
- She looks once, for a long time.
- Then never again, on purpose. Turning away from reflective surfaces becomes automatic.
- She doesn't ask where the face came from. The intel she can pick apart; the face she can't bring herself to question. (Canon: she only traces the biometric sale after Evelyn forces the issue.)

---

## Pulling the Records

- Rehab leaves her with idle time and nothing to do with it. She requests the after-action and intelligence records through proper channels.
- Her intent is professional, not suspicious: understand what enemy adaptation they missed so she'll recognize it next time.
- What she finds instead:
  - The capability _was_ reported, days before.
  - She traces it layer by layer: _unconfirmed_ → _low confidence_ → _residual_ → gone. The sector summary was marked stabilized for the milestone.
  - The overwatch position was selected on the strength of that summary.
- No lie. No villain. She finds the name of the last analyst in the chain and there's nothing to hate — just a person working a queue, rounding off.
- That's the worst part: nobody decided anything. The system wanted to be winning, and she was the rounding error. It echoes the "within acceptable parameters" she's already living inside.
- She files a formal report, because that's what she does. It's acknowledged and closed: **"Reviewed. No further action."**
- Her access to the records gets noticed. An automated wellness check-in lands on her schedule — duty of care. This time she understands what it is.
- No vow, no speech. A rule forms quietly: _intel is a claim, not a fact._

---

## Retraining

- Marksmanship with new eyes. Clumsy at first; her body and the feed disagree.
- The rangefinder is flawless. So she starts estimating range and wind herself _before_ looking at the readout, and logs both. (Origin of the habit in the present-day files: her own judgment as the backup when optics can be spoofed or damaged.)
- Trains every day, no exceptions. It's the one thing she controls end to end.
- Starts reading firmware notes and patch logs for her own implants. She refuses to be the only person who doesn't understand what she's seeing.
- What's left of the trauma, kept small and physical: a flinch at a certain pitch, bad sleep, a hand that goes to where the rifle should be. She functions anyway.
- Her hair is growing back. She decides not to cut it. It's the one part of her appearance that's coming back as hers.

---

## Reassignment

- Medically cleared.
- Not returned to her old squad — her billet is filled, and the squad has been rebuilt with people she doesn't know.
- Reassigned to Turquoise Nine as Senior Operative. The slight promotion reads as compensation. She notes it's the cheapest kind available.
- Arrives at the staging area, which Turquoise Nine is currently sharing with another squad. It's dusty; she has a respirator/dust mask up over her lower face — only her eyes and brow are visible.
- First brief: she reads the intel packet, then pulls the sources behind it and checks them line by line. The Field Officer notices. Within a day she's "the difficult one," and she doesn't care.
- She walks past a reflective window without looking.
- Final line: somewhere across the staging area, someone has stopped mid-sentence to stare at her. She doesn't notice. → hands off to her meeting with Evelyn.
