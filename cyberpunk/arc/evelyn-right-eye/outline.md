# Her Right Eye — Plot Beats

## Setup

**When:** Evelyn is 17, in the specialty year, breacher track. It's a few weeks after her reflex booster was installed, and the cohort ships out at 18.

**Where she is inside:** This is the dark period in her backstory. She's quietly seeking out pain and doesn't much care whether she wakes up tomorrow. None of that shows. On the surface she's exactly as loud, cheerful, and jokey as she is at 27.

**Squad:** Facet Four, her specialty-year training section. Training sections are named by gem-cutting stage: _Rough_ for the general academy years, _Facet_ for the specialty year. Recruits get a gem-numbered squad on assignment. Nobody gets a gem until they're finished being cut. The instructor is never named; the section calls them "Instructor," and Evelyn calls them nothing to their face.

**The booster:** New boosters have a calibration window. For a few weeks the booster and the nervous system are still learning each other. Techs warn trainees about occasional _stutters_, a dead half-beat where the booster and the body each wait for the other, and tell them to log every one. Evelyn has had two or three that week and hasn't logged any of them. They were interesting, and logging them was boring.

**Mira:** Evelyn's pair partner in breach drills and one of her real friends. She's a sweet girl who remembers everyone's birthday, saves Evelyn the good ration packs, apologizes too much, and gets nervous under evaluation because she wants so badly to do well. In breach pairs, Mira sets and fires the charge and Evelyn calls the count and goes in first.

**Core thread:** Losing the eye is a big deal, and for about a day Evelyn knows it. Then Mira needs it not to be one, so Evelyn keeps saying _it's just an eye_ until she believes it. She shrinks it in steps, each one reasonable: _it's bad_ → _it's fixable_ → _it's routine_ → _it's just an eye_ → _honestly, kind of an upgrade._ The argument works too well. If an eye is just a part, any part is just a part, and losing another one would probably be fine too. The blast doesn't take her self-preservation. She talks it away herself, one kind reassurance at a time, and never notices it's gone. (The ambiguity in canon, _may or may not_, comes from the dark period she was already in. She can't say whether the eye changed her or just finished something already under way.)

**The squad is vivid, deliberately:** Evelyn knows everyone's name, habits, and worst jokes. She's surrounded the whole time, and none of them hear what she's actually telling herself.

**POV:** Evelyn throughout.

**Tone:** The surface is bright and fast. The narration moves the way she talks, with tangents and jokes, cutting away whenever something gets heavy. The weight lives in what it skips. The first night after the injury is the one stretch where the jokes don't work yet. The injury is never graphic.

---

## The Drill — Everything's Fine

- Shoot-house breach-and-clear, timed and evaluated, with live training charges. It's loud, fun, and dangerous in a way she enjoys a little too much.
- Squad texture: names, nicknames, a running bet, someone's terrible singing. Evelyn is at the center of all of it.
- Mira before the run: nervous, rechecking the charge twice, asking Evelyn to do the count "normal speed this time." All week the booster has made Evelyn fast, and her count has been fast too. Mira has learned to anticipate it.
- Evelyn half-notices another small stutter during warm-up. She files it under _later_, which means never.
- Small hint of the dark underneath, left unexplained: she likes the drills that hurt. She has a bruise she keeps pressing on.

---

## The Breach

- They stack at the door. Mira sets the charge. Evelyn starts the count.
- Mira fires a beat early, on the rhythm she's learned to expect from boosted Evelyn, before the final call.
- The booster should snap Evelyn out of line. It stutters. There's no time to be afraid or to decide anything.
- The hit is small: a single fragment from the door at arm's length. The right side of the world switches off. Heat, a high sharp ringing, wet on her cheek. (Never described beyond sensation.)
- She's on the floor, and the first thing she does is joke. "Did we get the door?"
- Mira is screaming, then crying, then saying _sorry_ over and over. Evelyn sees her upside down, out of one eye.
- The corp vitals system has already flagged the injury. Instructors, a medic, hands. Evelyn keeps talking the whole way out, mostly to Mira: _hey, hey, it's fine, look at me, well, look at the left one._
- Dark.

---

## The First Night

- Medical: the eye can't be saved. They tell her replacement is routine, but that's tomorrow's problem.
- Tonight the right side is just gone. She keeps turning her head to find the missing half of the room, and it's never there.
- She's scared, plainly, the way she hasn't let herself be about anything in a long time. She liked that eye. It was hers.
- She tries a joke in her head, and it doesn't land. She tries another, and that doesn't land either.
- This is the only stretch in the arc where the narration can't cut away. Keep it short.

---

## Half the World — Recovery

- Morning: Mira arrives with red eyes, a smuggled pastry, and an apology she can't get through.
- Evelyn says it for the first time: _hey. It's just an eye._ She says it for Mira, but hearing herself say it helps. It gives her something to hold onto.
- So she keeps saying it. Mira comes every day, and every day Evelyn makes it smaller:
  - _They fix this all the time._
  - _I was getting bored of depth perception anyway._
  - _Implant bay's booked for the whole cohort, I'm just jumping the queue._
- One eye: she misjudges distances, knocks over cups, misses the edge of the bed, and turns every one into a bit.
- She's never alone. The squad visits in noisy waves. She spends her recovery looking after other people's feelings about her injury, which is easier than having her own.
- An automated wellness check-in arrives: _duty of care._ She aces it, cheerful and engaged, and gets flagged as _resilient._ By now her answers aren't even a performance. She mostly believes them.
- She catches herself pressing on the ache around the dressing and stops when someone looks. This is the older darkness, and it isn't explained.

---

## The Review

- A training-incident review takes statements. Evelyn makes sure she gives hers before Mira.
- Everything she says is true or close to it:
  - "Booster's been stuttering all week." (True.)
  - "I didn't log them." (True, and it costs her a reprimand. She takes it gladly.)
  - "I might've called the count early. Booster's been making me fast." (_Might've._ She deflects, but doesn't quite lie. Canon: lying is her last resort, reserved for personal matters, and this is personal.)
- The review is relieved. A hardware fault means a vendor ticket. A trainee error means a disciplinary track, a washout, and a cohort that ships short.
- Finding: **"Equipment calibration fault. Contributing factor: failure to report equipment anomalies."** Mira gets a minor procedural note and nothing more.
- Mira knows she fired before the final call. She tries to say so. Evelyn cuts her off, lightly and firmly: _it's closed. You want them to reopen it and wash you out over a door? Over an eye? It's just an eye, Mira._
- Mira lets it go, grateful and guilty. Evelyn feels good about this. It's the one thing in the arc she's sure she did right.
- She said the minimizing line to protect Mira in front of the review, and now it's on record. It's the official version, and a little more of it is true for Evelyn each time she says it.

---

## New Eye

- Implant surgery. The eye boots up with a calibration grid and diagnostic text. Evelyn is delighted.
- She asks whether it can do thermal, zoom, a glowing iris. It's standard issue, so no; anything more costs extra. She jokes about it and resolves to be rich someday.
- The last step: _honestly? Kind of an upgrade._ This time she isn't saying it for anyone else.
- The replacement cost goes onto her implant debt, which clears at four years of service. _I owe the corp an eye now._
- The tech matches the iris color from her left eye. In the mirror, she looks for a long time and can't tell which eye is the real one.
- She finds that funny. She winks at herself with the right one.

---

## The Log

- Booster recalibration session with a tech who likes the job and wants to talk about it.
- The tech pulls up the incident trace: "Want to see where it dropped? It's actually kind of interesting—"
- "Nah. It's done."
- She changes the subject to the tech's lunch, a rumor, anything at all. Going through it frame by frame would make it a big deal again, and she has worked hard to make it not one.

---

## Retraining

- Back in the shoot house. Mira's hands shake on the trigger whenever Evelyn is the one at the door.
- Mira can't look at Evelyn's eyes. Her gaze slides down every time.
- Evelyn asks for a different pair partner "so Mira can relax." She frames it as a kindness, and it is one. It's also a relief, and she doesn't examine that.
- At the door with the new partner, the thought arrives calmly, almost comfortingly: _worst case, I lose another piece. They'll give me a new one._ She believes it completely.
- She lets the booster drive and stops bracing. She's faster than she has ever been.
- Instructors: _whatever they did to that booster, it worked._ Her flinch is gone, and they log it as a calibration success. Nobody, Evelyn included, reads it any other way.

---

## Shipping Out

- Graduation at 18 and assignments. Mira is posted to a different squad.
- Mira: _promise you'll write?_ Evelyn: _obviously._ She doesn't say _promise_. (Canon: she takes promises seriously, which is why she rarely makes them.)
- Facet Four gets its assignments. Evelyn is posted to Cateye Seven.
- She reads it twice, and the joke comes right away: _CatEYE Seven, you've got to be kidding me,_ She keeps it to herself, because out loud it would land on Mira.
- Mira reads the posting over her shoulder, glances at Evelyn's right eye, and goes quiet. Evelyn grins and starts talking about something else.
- Final beat: at the transit point she walks past a reflective window, catches herself in it. She still can't tell which one is fake, and she has decided it doesn't matter. It's just a part.
- Hand-off: a few months later, someone else is walking around with the face she sold. (Not stated in the arc. It sets up her later meeting with Victoria.)

## After the arc

- **Mira afterward:** They drift apart gradually. Mira keeps writing, and Evelyn's replies get shorter and further apart. Every message carries Mira's guilt, and seeing it keeps the eye a big deal, which Evelyn can't afford after working so hard to make it small. Nobody ends it; it just thins out. (This is the same reasoning as refusing the trace and asking for a new pair partner.)
- **The later counterweight:** Canon says that at 27 she avoids injuring herself mostly because she hates seeing Victoria frightened. This arc is where that gap opens. Victoria is what later stands in for the self-preservation Evelyn talked herself out of.
