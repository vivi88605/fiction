# Second-in-Command on Crutches

The crutches announced her from forty meters out.

They were standard issue, grey alloy with a rubber foot that had worn down to the metal on one side, so every second step came with a small, precise *tick* against the barracks floor. *Tap, tick. Tap, tick.* It was not a loud sound. It did not need to be. By the time it turned the corner into Turquoise's bay, both gunners were standing at the ends of their bunks like they'd been bolted there, and the medic had stopped pretending to read.

Evelyn, on her bunk with one boot unlaced, decided that was a bit much and then stood up anyway.

Victoria came in slowly. That was the problem. At full speed an inspection from Victoria was a quick, efficient ordeal: she walked the line, her eyes did whatever her eyes did, she said three sentences, and it was over. At crutch speed she had time. She stopped at each bunk and just stood there, weight on the grips, and *looked*, and nobody in the bay could tell what she was looking at because the blue of those eyes didn't point anywhere in particular.

She stopped at the first gunner's kit for eleven seconds. Evelyn counted.

"Your magazine pouch," Victoria said.

The gunner looked down. The pouch looked fine.

Victoria lifted one crutch, hooked the tip under the pouch flap, and raised it half a centimeter. The snap underneath was cracked through. "That will open the first time you go prone. Replace it before rotation."

"Yes, Senior."

*Tap, tick.* The second gunner lasted four seconds. The medic's kit was clean, and she knew it, and she still exhaled when Victoria moved on.

Then Victoria got to Evelyn.

Evelyn had prepared. She had prepared *so* hard. Last night she'd laid out every piece of kit, checked it against the list, checked it again, and then, because she'd heard the crutches in the hall at 0540 the day before, checked it a third time with a torch. Her breaching charges sat in their foam at perfect right angles. Her boots, one lace aside, were cleaner than they'd been since the academy.

Victoria looked at it for a long time.

"Go on," Evelyn said. "Find something. I dare you."

"Your left boot is unlaced."

"I'm *wearing* that. That's not kit, that's me."

"You are also subject to inspection." Victoria's gaze stayed on the charges. Evelyn watched her notice the right angles, and watched her notice that she'd noticed, and for half a second something almost happened at the corner of Victoria's mouth. Then it didn't. "Acceptable."

Behind her, one of the gunners made a very quiet noise. Evelyn had never been called *acceptable* in her life, not even by the academy, which had a whole grading vocabulary for it.

"Did you hear that?" she said to the bay at large. "My hostile acquaintance Victoria just called me acceptable."

"I called your kit acceptable," Victoria said, already turning. "Lace your boot."

*Tap, tick. Tap, tick.* And she was gone, and the bay took a breath.

---

It had happened three days into refit, on the requalification course behind the vehicle sheds. It wasn't a raid or a firefight. It was the climbing wall, which Victoria had gone up a hundred times, and a handhold that had been bolted onto it by someone who wasn't thinking about the ninety-eighth person to put their weight on it. It sheared off in her hand at four meters.

Evelyn had been on the ground, bored, waiting her turn. She'd heard the hold crack before she saw anything, and then Victoria was down. She'd landed on her feet, mostly, and then her left leg had simply stopped agreeing with her.

She hadn't made a sound. That was what Evelyn remembered: the complete silence of it, Victoria sitting in the gravel with her face gone the color of paper and her jaw locked, saying "Don't," when Evelyn reached her, and then, "Don't touch the ankle," and then, very calmly, "It's broken. Call it in."

Tibia and fibula, just above the ankle. The CEF reinforced your skull and your ribs and your forearms and your spine, everything a blast or a round was likely to go for. Nobody reinforced your legs. Legs were cheap to fix.

Base medical set it, fitted a bone stimulator cuff around it that hummed faintly when the room was quiet, and gave her a return-to-duty date. The date was the morning Turquoise shipped out for the next rotation. Not a day earlier and not a day later, like the ankle had been told the schedule and was expected to comply.

"That's convenient," Evelyn had said, reading it over her shoulder.

"It isn't a coincidence," Victoria had said, and folded the sheet in half along a perfectly straight line.

---

Refit had a routine, and Victoria did not stop keeping it just because she had to swing through it on two sticks.

At 0600 every day she took her rifle case to the tech bay. It was a long walk at the best of times, across the whole base, past the motor pool and the mess and the long flat block where Sector Command's people worked with the blinds down. Refit was when optics got recalibrated and firmware got pushed and the tech-bay staff tried to rush her, and Victoria didn't let anyone rush her optics. The case weighed eleven kilos. Evelyn knew because she'd asked the armory, casually, the afternoon of the fall.

On the first morning after the fall, Evelyn was leaning on the wall outside Victoria's quarters at 0555 when the door opened.

Victoria stopped in the doorway. She had the crutches under her arms and the rifle case in one hand, gripping it against the crutch handle, which could not possibly work for more than ten meters.

"Morning," Evelyn said. She pushed off the wall, took the case out of Victoria's hand, and started walking.

Behind her, nothing. Then *tap, tick.*

"I didn't ask you to do that," Victoria said.

"Nope."

"I am capable of carrying my own equipment."

"Yep." Evelyn slowed down, since she'd gotten three meters ahead without meaning to, and fell into step at crutch pace, which was a strange, lurching rhythm she would have to learn. "Everyone knows. It's in your file. 'Senior Operative Victoria, capable of carrying her own equipment.' Top line."

Victoria did not answer that. Evelyn waited for the rest of it: the *give it back*, the *that's an order*, the flat look that meant Evelyn had stepped over a line she hadn't known was there. It didn't come. They crossed the motor pool. They passed the mess, where someone was already burning something. At the tech bay door Evelyn held it open with her hip, and Victoria went through without looking at her, and the tech on shift looked from the case in Evelyn's hand to the crutches and wisely said nothing either.

Evelyn set the case on the bench. Victoria unlatched it, checked the rifle was sitting exactly as she'd left it, and then checked again.

"Fifteen hundred," Evelyn said. "Yeah? I'll come back for it."

Victoria was already bent over the scope. "The calibration will take until fifteen-twenty."

That was not *no*. Evelyn went and got breakfast, feeling like she'd won something, and not totally sure what.

---

It became the routine.

Evelyn at 0555, the case, the walk. Victoria never asked and never thanked her, and Evelyn never brought it up, partly because she had a strong suspicion that the moment she said anything, it would stop. At fifteen-twenty she came back and carried it home. On the fourth day Victoria started giving her small technical corrections on the way ("you're swinging it; the mount doesn't like that") and Evelyn took that as a promotion.

Other things changed too. Nobody said anything about those either.

Victoria stopped taking the long way around the mess to avoid the queue, because the long way had a set of stairs. Evelyn started saving a seat at the end of the table, the one where you could put a leg out straight without anyone tripping over it, and on the fifth day Victoria sat in it. On the sixth she brought her tray to it before Evelyn had even got there. Evelyn stood in the queue with her own tray and pretended hard not to notice. It was the most self-control she'd ever shown.

Victoria was, if anything, *more* herself on crutches, not less. She ran the second kit inspection four days after the first, and this time she found a cracked optic seal on the medic's gear that the tech bay had missed. She corrected the Field Officer's route map for the next rotation's first staging area from a chair, with her leg up on another chair, and he just took the corrections. Evelyn watched her do her daily training too, since there was no world in which Victoria skipped daily training. Upper body only, grim and precise, pull-ups until her arms shook and then two more, the cuff on her ankle humming.

"You know you're allowed to rest," Evelyn said from the doorway of the gym.

"I am resting," Victoria said, hanging from the bar. "The leg is resting."

"That's the most you thing anyone's ever said."

Victoria dropped, landed on her good foot, and caught a crutch without looking for it. "Don't you have somewhere to be?"

"Nope," Evelyn said, and stayed.

---

On the ninth morning Evelyn was late.

It wasn't her fault. For once it really, truly wasn't. The Field Officer caught her at 0540 about a breaching plan that had come down from Sector Command with a door on it that didn't exist, and she spent eighteen minutes explaining to him that a wall with a door drawn on it was still a wall. She sprinted the length of the barracks block with her boots half-laced and came around the corner at 0613, ready to apologize.

Victoria's door was open.

Victoria was sitting on the end of her bunk just inside it, in uniform, crutches leaning against her knee, reading, probably, with her eyes on nothing in particular the way she did. The rifle case stood on the floor by the doorframe. It had been placed there deliberately, square to the wall, in the exact spot where Evelyn picked it up every morning.

And the handle was turned out, toward the corridor, so that someone coming from the direction of the Field Officer's office could take it without breaking stride.

Evelyn stopped.

Victoria's eyes came back from wherever they'd been. "You're late."

"Actual," Evelyn said. "Door that doesn't exist. Long story."

"I heard." Victoria reached for the crutches. "The calibration run is scheduled for 0615. We will be late."

*We.* Evelyn picked up the case. The handle was right where her hand went. She thought, very clearly: *She's been sitting there for eighteen minutes.* She could have gone without Evelyn. She could have had the tech bay send someone. She could have dragged it the whole way there on a crutch just to prove she could, and she'd have *enjoyed* that, a little.

She'd sat on her bunk and put the case by the door and waited.

Evelyn had about nine different jokes lined up. Every single one of them would have made Victoria walk the case to the tech bay herself if it took all morning.

"Right," she said. "Let's go."

*Tap, tick.* They crossed the motor pool. Somebody was burning something at the mess again. Victoria said, "You're swinging it," and Evelyn said, "Sorry, sorry," and stopped swinging it, and did not say a single thing about the handle for the rest of her life. As far as she was concerned, it beat the mess-hall seat by a mile.

---

Victoria was cleared on the morning of departure, as scheduled. The cuff came off. She walked out of base medical on her own two legs with a slight care in the left one that anyone else would have missed and Evelyn, who had spent three weeks learning her rhythm, didn't.

At the transport, Turquoise loaded in the grey half-light: gunners first, then the medic's crates, then the charges in their foam. Victoria came up the ramp last with the rifle case in her own hand, where it belonged, and Evelyn didn't reach for it. She had decided that last night. Three weeks was three weeks. You didn't make a thing of it.

At the top of the ramp, Victoria's left foot hit the lip wrong, just slightly. It wasn't enough for anyone to see.

Evelyn's hand went out on its own.

And Victoria let go of the handle. She didn't hand it over or look at her or say a word. She just opened her fingers a half-second before Evelyn's closed, so that the case was never, for one instant, not being held by someone. Then she walked to her seat, sat down, and began checking her straps.

Evelyn stood in the aisle holding eleven kilos of someone else's rifle, feeling like an idiot and having a wonderful time.

She stowed it in the rack above Victoria's seat, square to the wall, handle out.

"My friend Victoria—" she started, dropping into the seat beside her.

"No," said Victoria, and closed her eyes. She did not tell Evelyn to move.
