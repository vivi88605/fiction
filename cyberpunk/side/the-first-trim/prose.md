# The First Trim

The pump station was the most boring thing Concord owned. Evelyn had decided this at about midnight, and by oh-three-hundred she had evidence.

It sat in the valley below them, humming, lit up white. Some contract had decided it was critical infrastructure, so Turquoise had been posted on the ridge above it for three nights, watching nothing happen to it. The rest of the squad was down in the vehicle laager, asleep in shifts. Victoria had the hide. Evelyn had the job of lying two meters behind Victoria with a carbine, making sure nobody walked up the back of the ridge and stabbed her. That had been very exciting for the first eleven minutes.

So Evelyn watched Victoria instead, which was at least a moving target.

Mostly Victoria didn't move. She lay along the rifle like she'd been bolted to it, and every few minutes she'd murmur a range or a wind correction into the comm. Nobody needed it, so it was practice, or a prayer, or both. But every so often her left hand came up off the stock and raked her fringe sideways off her forehead, and then the hand went back, and a little while later the fringe slid back down into her eyes, and then the hand came up again.

Evelyn had gotten to fourteen.

"I could cut that," she said on the squad channel, on the private side.

"No."

"After. Not now. I'm not a monster."

"No," Victoria said, and pushed her hair out of her face for the fifteenth time.

---

The thing about the hair was that it had a history, and everybody in Turquoise knew it the way you knew about weather that had already happened.

Victoria had arrived at the squad with about two centimeters of it, pale and dead level all over her head, every strand the same length because it had all started growing back on the same day. Nearly a year since. Now it was past her ears and in a stub of a tail at the back that wouldn't stay in its elastic, and at the front it came down past her brows. Right into the place where a scope picture was supposed to be.

At the rear base, three days into the refit, Evelyn walked into the hygiene block and found Victoria standing in front of the groomer.

The groomer was a booth with a padded seat and a hood that came down over your head, and a panel on the side that offered three regulation profiles. They were called _Standard_, _Standard Short_, and _Standard Field_, and as far as Evelyn could tell they were the same crop in three moods. It did the job in under a minute, then logged the result to your appearance record so the checkpoint cameras didn't have a crisis about your new hair.

Victoria was reading the panel. She read the whole thing, the way she read everything, including the small print about consent to biometric update. Then she turned around and walked out past Evelyn without stopping.

Evelyn caught up with her in the corridor.

"You know you looked good with it short," she said. "When you got here. I'm only saying it because someone should."

"Nobody should."

"It was a whole look. Very—" There was a word there, and she stepped around it like a puddle. "Very _operational_."

"No."

"I wasn't asking anything. That was a compliment. You can't no a compliment."

"No," Victoria said. Evelyn decided to count it anyway.

---

The third time, she caught Victoria at it.

It was late, the tech bay was empty, and the only light was the task lamp over Victoria's bench. Her rifle was in pieces on a cloth, laid out in the order it came apart. Victoria was sitting in the middle of all that order, with her eyes shut, holding the little scissors from her multitool up to her own fringe.

By feel.

With her eyes shut.

Evelyn crossed the bay faster than she'd crossed most doorways, took the multitool out of Victoria's hand, and folded it shut.

"Absolutely not."

Victoria opened her eyes. "Give that back."

"You were going to cut your own eyelid off."

"I was going to cut a straight line."

"With a _multitool_. Vic. Those are for zip ties." Evelyn held it up out of reach, which didn't really work, because Victoria had nine centimeters on her. Victoria didn't try to take it, though. She just looked at it, and then at the rifle on the cloth, and then at nothing in particular. "There's a mirror in the hygiene block, if you have to—"

She heard it as she said it, and she stopped. Victoria's face didn't change, and that was the answer. Evelyn had brushed her teeth next to Victoria for long enough to know where Victoria looked at those sinks, which was the tap.

"Let me," Evelyn said. "Just the front."

"No."

Three.

Evelyn put the multitool on the bench, closed, and slid it back across. She didn't leave. She sat on the next stool over and started talking about the protein loaf, which tonight had been labeled _Savory Roast_ and had tasted like the label. And Victoria reassembled the rifle with her fringe in her eyes, pushing it aside every time she bent over a part.

When the bolt carrier was back in, Victoria said, without looking up, "The front."

Evelyn didn't say anything. She wasn't completely sure that if she opened her mouth she wouldn't ruin it.

"Only the front," Victoria said. "Tomorrow. Twenty-one hundred. Here."

---

The tech bay had good light, the only good light on the base. Evelyn brought her scissors in their case, the expensive ones, Japanese steel with the little offset handle. Money-wise they were the worst decision she'd ever made, and as far as she was concerned the best one. She brought a comb and a spray bottle she'd stolen from the medic. She also brought a towel that turned out to be unnecessary, because Victoria had brought her own, folded into a square, and was holding it on her knees.

There was a bank of diagnostic monitors along the back wall. Most of them were dark. Black glass, dark enough to show your face back to you. Evelyn put the stool down facing the other way, toward the bench and the lamp, and didn't say anything about it.

Victoria sat down. "You will tell me before every cut."

"Every cut?"

"Every cut."

"Sure, but there's going to be a lot of them."

"Then there will be a lot of them."

Evelyn sprayed the fringe damp and combed it down. Wet, it came all the way to the bridge of Victoria's nose. The comb's teeth crossed the brow, and Victoria went still under it. It was the same still she went on the rifle: a breath let out and then nothing moving. Her hands were flat on the folded towel, pressing into it hard enough that the fold had gone crooked.

"You can shut your eyes," Evelyn said. "It gets everywhere."

Victoria didn't shut them.

Fine. Evelyn slid her first two fingers under the fringe and laid them flat across Victoria's forehead, the hair caught between them, and drew them down to where the line should go. You cut along the fingers, with the blades on the outside. That was the method. That was how she did her own fringe, and it was the right way to cut a straight line, and that was the only reason. It had nothing to do with her fingers being between the blades and anything else in particular.

"Cutting."

Snip. A pale little crescent of hair dropped onto the towel. Victoria breathed in, finally.

"Cutting." Snip. "Here's a thing. Is hair alive?"

"No."

"But it grows."

"It grows from the follicle. The follicle is alive. What you are holding is product."

"_Product_. Your head has output. Cutting." Snip. "Is that true for everybody, or just you?"

"Everyone's head has output."

"Mine's mostly noise."

"Yes," Victoria agreed.

Evelyn went slow. She never went slow on her own. Her own took two minutes in front of whatever reflective thing was nearest. This took longer than Victoria's rifle maintenance. She took a few hairs at a time, combed it down again after every pass, moved her fingers along half a centimeter, laid them flat again, said _cutting_. She hummed some of the time, without meaning to, and stopped when she noticed.

"You know there's a guy in Sector," she said, "whose whole job is updating your appearance record. And tomorrow he gets a notification. _Needle, fringe, revised._ He has to open it. He has to have an opinion."

"It's automated."

"There's always a guy. Cutting."

Somewhere around the middle, Victoria shut her eyes.

Evelyn didn't see it happen. She looked down to set the next line and they were just shut, lashes down, the lids flickering a little the way they did when Victoria was reading something on the inside of them. Her hands on the towel had stopped pressing.

Evelyn did one cut without announcing it, and Victoria didn't say anything, and Evelyn didn't push her luck with a second.

"While I'm up here," she said, after that, "I could take the back in too. Make it even. All the way back to how you had it."

"No."

"I'm serious. It really was a good look."

"It was shaved," Victoria said.

"I know, but—"

"For surgery, Evelyn."

The scissors stopped open, a centimeter off her own knuckle.

"Yeah," Evelyn said. "I know." And then, because it was true and she couldn't help it: "It grew back good, though."

"The front."

"The front. Cutting."

The thing she'd never told anyone, and was never going to, was that she'd seen Victoria for about half a second before she _saw_ her. At the staging area, before the transfer, before any of it. A woman had walked past Cateye's ready room with her head cropped close and even, and a jaw set like a shut door, and Evelyn's first thought hadn't been _wait, what the fuck_. Her first thought had been _oh. Handsome._

Then the rest of the face had arrived, and the thought had become unusable. Anything nice she said about that face, she was saying about her own, and she had plenty of jokes about her own face. Loud ones. She used them on everybody. None of them could be used here.

But the jaw had always been Victoria's. The mouth, too. The crop had put all of that up front. And the hair coming down around it now meant Victoria read, from a distance, as less like anyone in particular. Evelyn had a theory about that. She wasn't going to finish it while she had scissors in her hand.

"Cutting," she said.

The left side came out long, the way it always did on her own, because she was right-handed and the angle was bad. She took it up to match. Then she combed everything down one more time, ran a thumb under the line to check it, put the scissors on the bench, and stepped back to look.

Oh.

The fringe sat just above the brows, choppy at the ends, because that was how Evelyn cut hers. It was a little longer at the outside corners, because that was what Evelyn's hands did when they weren't watching themselves, and her hands had been watching everything except that. Below it was Victoria's jaw and Victoria's mouth. Above it, with nothing in the way anymore, was the top half of a face Evelyn had been looking at in mirrors her whole life.

"Done," she said. "Gorgeous. I'm a genius. You can open your eyes."

Victoria opened them. She lifted the towel off her lap by its corners, folding it in on itself so no hair fell on the floor. She touched the fringe with two fingertips, along the line from one side to the other, the way she'd check a rail for level. Then she stood up, and turned around to put the stool back.

Behind her, the diagnostic bank had gone dark. Every screen on it had timed out while Evelyn was cutting. Lit from the front by the task lamp, they had turned into a long wall of black glass, and in the black glass there were two of them.

Victoria stopped.

Evelyn had seen her stop like that exactly once before, at a different piece of reflective glass, a year ago, and she'd been told to leave Victoria alone right after it. She stood very still and got ready to be told it again.

Victoria didn't say anything. She looked. She didn't look the way she looked at a tap, either. She looked straight at it, and for a long time, longer than Evelyn had ever seen her give her own face. In the glass, the two fringes fell exactly the same way. Their brows sat under them at the same height, with the same blue underneath, three of the eyes the same and one not. Further down, the faces went separate ways, Victoria's jaw and Evelyn's chin. Evelyn was in the reflection too, behind Victoria's shoulder, and she could see herself watching Victoria look. She looked like someone waiting to find out if they'd been caught.

"You cut it like yours," Victoria said.

There were several answers. A couple of them were even jokes. Evelyn picked the true one.

"It's the only way I know how."

Victoria kept looking. Then she reached up and pushed at the fringe once with the back of her knuckle, the way she'd been doing for a week. This time it didn't go anywhere. It was already out of the way.

"The checkpoint will flag me tomorrow," she said.

"For the haircut?"

"For you. Sector will think it's a clerical error. They'll merge our files." There was no change at all in her voice. "I'll inherit your inclusions."

It took Evelyn a full second to understand that Victoria had made a joke, about the face, out loud. The laugh came out of her like something had been knocked loose. "You'd _hate_ my inclusions. There's one for _tone_."

"I know. I've read them."

"You've _read_ my—"

"I am your second-in-command," Victoria said, and turned away from the glass. She put the stool back where it belonged, square to the bench. She tucked the folded towel under her arm. She got as far as the door.

"Four weeks," she said.

"What?"

"It will need doing again in four weeks. Before the rotation." She looked back. Her face was very much the same face. "Same method."

Then she was gone, and Evelyn was alone in the tech bay with a comb in one hand and Victoria's hair all over her sleeves, and the wall of black glass showing her one person now instead of two. She didn't run down the corridor after Victoria to point out that she'd just been booked for a standing appointment. Seeing the glass first and then booking it, in that order. She didn't say a word about it to anyone. It was possibly the most heroic thing she'd ever done, and nobody would ever give her an inclusion for it, or whatever the opposite was.

She did put it in her calendar. Four weeks out, twenty-one hundred, tech bay.

It was the first thing in there that wasn't an order.

---

The checkpoint at the range didn't flag her. Victoria noted that. The system had updated without asking anyone, which was what systems did.

The reshoot was at oh-eight-hundred. She shot it clean. Nothing came down into the picture, and she made the two points back plus one she hadn't lost. She logged it. She also logged, separately and for no one, that she had not pushed her hair aside once. That was a measurable improvement with a known cause, and recording known causes was good practice.

The medic passed her in the corridor afterward, slowed, looked at her, and said, "Huh."

"Evelyn cut it," Victoria said.

"Yeah," said the medic, and looked a second longer. "I can see that."

The likeness was very visible. Victoria had established that the night before, at some length, and it didn't need establishing again. The cut was what it was. It had been done by the only method the person doing it knew, and it was level, and it held, and there was no operational reason to change it.

What she had noticed in the glass, besides the obvious, was Evelyn. She had been standing behind Victoria's shoulder with her hands not quite at her sides, braced. It was the way she braced on the near side of a door before it went. She had expected to be sent away. Victoria had assessed that, standing at the glass, and found she didn't want to send her away. That was a finding. She had filed it, the way she filed findings she didn't yet have a use for.

There was also the word Evelyn had stepped around in the corridor three days ago. _Very—_, and then something else in its place. Victoria had noted the gap at the time. She'd heard it again last night, in nearly the same place. She had not asked. It was the kind of question that would get a real answer, and she wasn't sure what she'd do with one.

She put the next trim in her own calendar, four weeks out, and a reminder three days before that, because Evelyn would forget.

Then, after a moment, she deleted the reminder. Evelyn wouldn't.
