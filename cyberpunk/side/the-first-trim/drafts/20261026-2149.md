# The First Trim

The fourth time Victoria pushed her hair out of her face, Evelyn started counting.

It was requalification week at the rear base, which was the dead middle of a refit: Concord had finished checking that your implants worked and hadn't started checking anything else yet. Turquoise had the long lane from fourteen hundred. The gunners had shot their tables and left. The medic had shot hers and left. Evelyn had shot hers too, technically, with a pistol at twenty-five meters, and passed with a score the range officer had described as "committed." She'd stayed anyway, because the bench behind lane six had a heating vent under it, and because Victoria was on lane six.

Victoria shot prone the way she did everything else, as if someone was going to grade the setup separately from the result. Mat squared to the lane. Magazines in a row. Rifle settled, cheek down, a long breath out, a pause just long enough that Evelyn always thought she'd fallen asleep, and then the shot.

And then, between that shot and the next one, Victoria's left hand would come off the rifle and drag her fringe sideways across her forehead. It stayed there for about as long as it took her to get back on the glass. Then it slid back down.

Five. Six.

Everyone in Turquoise knew about the hair, in the sense that it had happened in front of them. Victoria had arrived at the squad with about two centimeters of it, pale and dead even and very obviously grown back all at once. In the year since, nobody had stopped it. Now it was past her ears, and at the back it went into a tail so short it was mostly elastic. At the front it came down past her brows in a long uneven fringe, right where a scope picture would be.

Seven. Victoria said something under her breath that wasn't a swear, because Victoria didn't swear.

Eight came mid-sight. Victoria came off the rifle completely, sat back on her heels, and pressed her whole palm flat against her forehead, as if she was checking for a fever.

"I could cut that," Evelyn said.

"No."

Evelyn started a second column.

The score came up on the lane display a minute later. Victoria looked at it for a long time. It was, by any standard Evelyn had ever held herself to, an insane score.

"Two points," Victoria said.

"Down from what? Perfect?"

"Two points."

She knew exactly where they'd gone. Evelyn knew too. They'd gone into the hair, about a quarter point a push, and Victoria filled out the form at the end of the lane in her small square handwriting and requested a reshoot. A reshoot. To get two points back on a qualification she'd already passed by a distance you could land a transport in.

Evelyn loved her a little bit for that, in the hostile-acquaintance way.

---

At evening mess Victoria showed up with her fringe clipped back off her forehead with a small black binder clip, the kind the techs used to hold cable bundles together during a repair.

Evelyn put her tray down very carefully so she wouldn't drop it.

"Don't," Victoria said.

"I didn't say anything."

"You are about to."

"I was going to say it's very practical. Very field-expedient. If we ever need to bundle you, we'll know where to start." Evelyn sat across from her. Dinner was the protein loaf in the flavor the menu board called *Savory*, which was the same as the flavor it called *Hearty*, which was the same as last Tuesday. "Does it hurt?"

"It's a temporary measure."

"So's the war."

Victoria's mouth did a thing. It wasn't a smile, she didn't do those in public, but it was a thing, and Evelyn counted it for herself. Then the clip slipped a centimeter down one side and Victoria reached up and pushed it back without looking. She'd have to take it off under a helmet anyway. The helmet's padding would sit right on it, and she'd be lying on the range tomorrow with a binder clip denting her skull, shooting two points better and with a headache.

"I could do it," Evelyn said. "Just the front. Ten minutes."

"No."

"I cut mine."

Victoria looked at Evelyn's hair, which was doing what Evelyn's hair always did, which was standing up in the back where she'd slept on it. "I can tell."

"That's not a no, that's a review."

"No," Victoria said, and went back to the loaf.

Two.

There was also the groomer, which Evelyn didn't mention because it wasn't worth mentioning. It was a booth in the hygiene block with a padded seat, a hood that came down over your head, and three regulation profiles, all of them a crop. It did the job in forty seconds and logged the new cut to your appearance record so that the recognition system wouldn't flag you at the next checkpoint for having gotten a haircut. It could put Victoria back to two centimeters before she'd finished sitting down. Evelyn had never seen her go near it.

There was also the hygiene block mirror, which Evelyn didn't mention either. She'd stood next to Victoria at those sinks enough mornings now to know where Victoria looked when she brushed her teeth, which was at the tap.

---

Evelyn did her own that night, partly because it needed doing, but mostly to make a point.

She did it on the end of her bunk with the door to the squad room open and a hand mirror wedged against her knee. She used her scissors, the good ones. Japanese steel, a little offset handle, a leather case with a snap. They'd cost more than her boots. Then again, she hadn't paid for her boots, Concord had, so that didn't really tell you much. Mostly it meant they were the single most expensive thing she owned that wasn't installed in her.

She combed her fringe flat, caught it between two fingers, and cut along the line of them. Snip, shift, snip. She'd been cutting her own hair since she was twelve, since around the time she'd worked out that a pair of kitchen scissors and some nerve cost less than a barber. It took about two minutes. At the end she shook her head like a dog and let it fall wherever it wanted, which was the whole point of her hair.

Victoria was at the table across the room with the squad's maintenance log, which was what Victoria did with free evenings. She hadn't looked up once. She was also, Evelyn was almost sure, watching.

"See?" Evelyn said. "Two minutes. Haven't lost an eye yet." Then, before her brain could catch up with her mouth: "Not to scissors, anyway."

That one hadn't been meant for out loud. It hung there for a second. Victoria looked up, finally. Her face did nothing at all, which on Victoria was a lot.

"No," Victoria said, and looked back down at the log.

Three. Evelyn turned the mirror face-down on the blanket, because it was pointing at the table, and went to find someone in the corridor to bother.

---

At twenty-one hundred the next night Victoria appeared at the end of her bunk with a folded towel, a comb, and the stool from the ready room.

"The front," she said. "Only the front."

Evelyn sat up so fast she hit her head on the bunk above. "Yeah?"

"The reshoot is at oh-eight-hundred."

That was clearly the whole explanation, as far as Victoria was concerned, and Evelyn didn't ask for more of one. She got the scissors out of her locker. Victoria put the stool down in the middle of the floor, facing the lockers, whose doors had been polished by about a decade of bored recruits until they were nearly as good as a mirror. Evelyn picked the stool up, turned it around so it faced the window, and put it back down.

"Light's better," she said.

The light was not better. The window looked out over the vehicle yard, which was lit by one flood that flickered. Victoria sat down without a word.

"Terms," Victoria said.

"Oh, there are terms."

"You will tell me before every cut."

"Every cut?"

"Every cut."

"That's going to take a long time."

"Then it will take a long time."

"Fine. Terms accepted. Anything else? Safe word?"

Victoria looked at her.

"Okay," Evelyn said. "No safe word."

She wet the comb at the sink in the corner and combed the fringe down flat over Victoria's forehead. It was longer than it looked when it was hanging. Combed straight, it came down to the bridge of Victoria's nose. When the comb's teeth passed over her brow, Victoria went still in a way Evelyn recognized from the long lane. Breath out, and then nothing moving at all. Her hands were flat on her knees, fingers spread and pressing down.

"You can close your eyes," Evelyn said. "Hair goes everywhere."

Victoria didn't.

Okay. Evelyn slid her first two fingers under the fringe and pressed them flat against Victoria's forehead, just above the brows, so the hair lay between them, and drew them down to where she wanted the line. That was how you cut a fringe. Fingers flat, blades on the far side, cut along the fingers. You did it that way because it gave you a straight line. That was the reason. It was how she did her own, and it was simply the correct method. It had nothing to do with which side of her fingers the blades were on, and which side Victoria's eyes were on.

"Cutting," she said.

Snip.

A little fringe of pale hair dropped onto the towel over Victoria's shoulders. Victoria breathed in again, finally.

"Cutting."

Snip.

"Did you know hair's dead?" Evelyn said. "Like, all of it. The part you can see. It's just dead stuff your head keeps pushing out. I'm not cutting you, I'm doing a little light corpse disposal. Cutting."

Snip.

"The follicle is alive," Victoria said. Her voice was very level. "The shaft is keratinized cells. Dead is not the right category."

"What's the right category?"

Victoria considered it. Evelyn could feel her considering it, a slight shift of her forehead against Evelyn's fingers as her brows drew in. "Product," she said.

"Your head has *output*."

"Everyone's head has output."

"Cutting. I'm going to think about that for the rest of my life. Cutting."

She went slowly. She didn't usually. Her own fringe took two minutes and this was taking the kind of time Victoria spent setting up a shot, a few hairs at a time, combing it down again between each pass, moving her fingers a centimeter along and pressing them flat again before every single cut. If Victoria noticed, she didn't say. If she had, Evelyn had an answer ready: *I'm an artist, artists are slow, ask anyone who's ever commissioned one.*

"Somewhere in Sector," Evelyn said, "there's a guy whose whole job is your appearance record. And tomorrow he gets a notification. *Needle. Fringe. Revised.* He has to go and look at it. He has to approve it. Cutting."

"There is no guy. It's automated."

"There's always a guy. Cutting."

Somewhere around the middle, Victoria closed her eyes.

Evelyn didn't see it happen. She looked down to set the next line, and they were just closed, lashes down, the lids not quite still. Victoria's hands were still flat on her knees, but they weren't pressing anymore.

Evelyn didn't say anything for one whole cut. She didn't announce that cut either, and Victoria didn't call her on it.

"You know," she said, on the one after, "while I'm up here, I could take the back in too. Even it out. Take it all the way back to how you had it when you got here."

"No."

She'd stopped counting a while ago. That column had gotten long.

"I'm serious. It was a really good look."

"No."

"It was very—" There was a word there. She went around it. "—second-in-command. Very regulation. It said, *I will be correcting your route plan*."

"It was shaved," Victoria said.

"I know. It—"

"For surgery."

Evelyn's scissors stopped, open, a centimeter from her own fingers.

"Yeah," she said. Then: "It grew back good, though."

"The front, Evelyn."

"The front. Cutting."

Snip.

The thing was, she'd seen Victoria for about half a second before she'd seen her. That was the part she'd never told anybody and wasn't planning to. Back at the staging area, before the transfer, before the reflective glass and *similarity is not provenance* and any of it, a woman had walked past the Cateye ready room with her hair cropped to two even centimeters and a jaw like a door someone had shut on purpose. And Evelyn's first thought had not been *wait, what the fuck*. Her first thought had been *oh. Handsome.*

Then the rest of the face had arrived, and the thought had become unusable. Anything nice she said about that face, she was saying about her own, and she had a lot of jokes about her own face. She used them all the time. None of them were jokes she could tell Victoria.

But the jaw had always been Victoria's. The mouth too. The crop had put all of it out in the open. The hair now was coming down and in around it, and grown out, from the side, Victoria read as a lot less like anybody in particular, and Evelyn had a theory about that. She was not going to finish it while she was holding scissors.

"Cutting," she said.

The last pass was the left side, where it always came out a little long on her own, because she was right-handed and the angle was bad. She took it up to match. Then she combed everything down one more time, ran her thumb along the line to check it, set the scissors down on the windowsill, and stepped back.

Oh.

The fringe sat just above Victoria's brows, the way Evelyn's sat. It was cut a little choppy at the ends, because Evelyn cut it choppy at the ends. It was a few millimeters longer at the outside corners, because that was what Evelyn's fingers did when they weren't paying attention, and her fingers had been paying attention to everything except that. Below it was Victoria's jaw and Victoria's mouth. Above it, now that there was nothing in the way, was about half a face Evelyn had seen every morning of her life.

Victoria still had her eyes closed. She didn't know yet. There was nothing reflective in front of her except the window, and the window was mostly flood glare.

"Done," Evelyn said. "Gorgeous. Ten out of ten. Tell all your friends. You don't have any friends, tell your hostile acquaintances."

Victoria opened her eyes. She lifted the towel off her shoulders carefully, folding it in on itself so that none of the hair fell on the floor. Then she reached up and touched the ends of the fringe with two fingertips, along the whole line from one side to the other, the way she ran a hand along a rail to check it was level.

Then she brought both hands up in front of her, in the shape of a rifle that wasn't there, put her cheek down on nothing, and looked along it at the far end of the squad room.

She held it for a breath. Nothing slid down.

"Acceptable," she said.

"Acceptable? I'm an artist."

Victoria stood, picked up the stool, tucked the folded towel under her arm. She got as far as the door and stopped.

"Four weeks," she said.

"What?"

"It will need doing again in four weeks. Before the rotation."

And then she was gone into the corridor with the stool. Evelyn stood in the middle of the room with a comb in her hand and a lot of very short blond hair stuck to her sleeves, and did not run after her and say anything at all about the fact that Victoria had just booked her a standing appointment. It was possibly the hardest thing she'd ever done. Being shot at had been easier.

---

At the reshoot in the morning, Victoria didn't touch her hair once. She got the two points back, and one more she hadn't lost.

The medic passed them in the corridor afterward. She looked at Victoria, then at Evelyn, then at Victoria again, slowed down for a step like she was about to say something, and then didn't, and kept walking.

Victoria didn't seem to notice. Victoria was reading her score off her own eyes, which Evelyn could tell because she was walking a little too straight, the way she did when she was reading.

Evelyn put the scissors back in the bottom of her locker, in their case, wrapped in a sock, where nobody doing an inspection would think to look. Then she opened her calendar and put it in, four weeks out, twenty-one hundred.

It was the first thing in there that wasn't an order.
