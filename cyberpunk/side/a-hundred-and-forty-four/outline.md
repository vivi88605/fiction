# A Hundred and Forty-Four

Evelyn's POV, close third. A routine retrieval job goes wrong at the one point where Victoria is weakest: her own exit, alone. Victoria breaks her right leg and right wrist and keeps working anyway, mostly to send Evelyn away from her. Evelyn has to override the voice she always obeys on comms, get her out, and then run everything Victoria normally runs.

## Setup

- Routine retrieval through Dell: a drive from a parts crew's warehouse that's been brokering data on the side. Small operator, cameras, a lock, a couple of people on site. No Carol, so it's not worth her cut.
- Night, light rain. Victoria is on the roof of a condemned tenement about 280m out, drone up. Evelyn is inside.
- Dell's pitch left out a spotter on the warehouse roof with a cheap thermal monocular. Victoria flags a stationary heat source early and logs it as probably a vent. It isn't.

## Beats

1. **Objective phase.** Evelyn has the drive and is happy, heart rate high. Victoria is calling time. Victoria re-checks the "vent" and it turns its head toward her roof.
2. **Victoria made.** A car leaves the lot for her building. Victoria orders Evelyn onto her exit route and starts breaking down. Two men are entering her building by the north stair, so she takes the fire escape on the alley side.
3. **The fall.** A corroded bracket shears. Over comms Evelyn hears metal, impact, and one sound cut short. Victoria's live vitals spike. The silence that follows is Victoria dragging herself and the rifle case behind the bins before she says a word: the noise was loud, the men are directly overhead, and anything left in the open gives her away.
4. **The report.** Victoria's reports flat and fast in the old CEF casualty format: status, injuries, position, threats. Right lower leg broken, right wrist broken, can't walk. They heard the fall and are looking over the edge. From up there it's only a broken fire escape in the dark, and they can't tell whether she went down and ran. Then: "Your route's still clean. Take it."
   - How Evelyn knows how bad it is: Victoria reporting in the drill-perfect format. Training is what comes out of her when everything else drops away.
5. **The plan.** Evelyn asks what Victoria does if she leaves. Victoria has an answer, because she always has a fallback: stay behind the bins and let them decide she ran. Evelyn gets the car clear, then comes back for her once they've given up. Evelyn asks what happens if they check the alley. A pause too long for Victoria: "Then I'm behind the bins with a pistol."
   - Victoria intends to live. She's betting on bad odds she'd never let Evelyn take, and she knows it.
   - To her it's triage: if Evelyn leaves, one person is certainly safe; if Evelyn comes, she crosses open ground with nobody watching her back, the one job Victoria can't do from here. Under the arithmetic is the thing she can stand least, which is bracing for Evelyn's death because Evelyn ran toward her.
6. **The override.** Evelyn judges the plan bad, not just disobeys it. She plays the only card she has: then she'll find the way herself, slower and louder. Victoria gives her the route, because Evelyn coming carelessly is worse than Evelyn coming guided. She's still flying the drone, still calling movement, from the bottom of an alley.
   - Up top, they split: one man comes down the stair to check the alley, one stays on the roof, one works the street.
7. **The alley.** Evelyn arrives just as the man checking the alley finds Victoria with his torch. Victoria has her pistol in her left hand, which is the wrong one. A second man comes out of the stair door behind him. Close range is Evelyn's territory. Booster, short and ugly, and it's over. She runs the booster too hard and goes dizzy.
8. **The carry.** Victoria is taller, heavier, and the stronger of the two. Evelyn is built for bursts. Hopping is too slow, so it's a fireman's carry, with Evelyn counting steps out loud to the car. Victoria says to leave the rifle case. Evelyn leaves it, gets Victoria in the car, then reverses into the alley mouth and grabs it while Victoria tells her exactly what she thinks of that.
9. **Jax's.** He sets both breaks. The wrist broke just past where her forearm reinforcement ends ("very precise of you"). He offers a bone-knit brace that cuts healing from about eight weeks to three or four. Evelyn says yes before Victoria can finish working out the cost, and pays it from her own cut. Jax and Evelyn joke, but one of Evelyn's comes out too dark and Jax lets it go. Jax checks Evelyn's eyes for booster strain. Victoria, flat on a table and full of painkillers, still clocked Evelyn's gait coming in.
   - Victoria's first real question to Jax: how long until she can shoot.
10. **Home: the reset.** Evelyn works through Victoria's after-job list without being asked: she confirms payment and leans on Dell about the spotter he didn't mention, gets Victoria's injuries settled, and cleans the gear. She cleans the rifle at the kitchen table. Victoria starts instructing from the couch, then stops, because Evelyn has watched her do it for years and knows every step.
11. **Close.** Late. Neither has switched off live vitals since the job. Victoria notices first. "You haven't turned off live." "Neither have you." Neither does.

## Intent

- Role reversal on both axes: Evelyn becomes outside (the big picture, the logistics, the steadiness), and Victoria becomes the one who can't move and has to be carried.
- Victoria's coping is to keep working. Ordering Evelyn away is the most Victoria act in the piece, and it's care.
- Evelyn's fear runs under the jokes the whole way. Caretaking gives her motion, which is how she copes.
- Close to the unspoken things (Victoria bracing, Evelyn being frightened for her) but neither gets said outright.
