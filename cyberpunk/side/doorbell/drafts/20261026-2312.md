# Doorbell

The job was a drive. Not even an interesting drive. Dell had said "sensitive" four times in the pitch, which from Dell meant somebody's embarrassing spreadsheets, and the somebody had left them in a desk on the third floor of a building that leased office space to six companies. By Victoria's count, at least four of the six didn't exist.

"Mag lock on the stairwell door," Victoria said in the car, for the second time. "Old model. The device will hold it. Don't touch the handle until it's green."

"Green, handle. Got it."

"Don't touch the handle until it's green."

"That's three times now, I'm counting."

"Two. The first one was the briefing." Victoria checked the rifle case's latches, which she'd already checked in the garage, then got out of the car. "Comms in ninety seconds."

Evelyn hummed while she waited. Nothing in particular. It was a nice night, as nice as the city managed, which meant the rain had stopped and the smog had settled low enough to make the streetlights look soft. A billboard across the road was trying to sell her a mattress that tracked her sleep. She already had one of those; it was called Victoria.

The comm clicked open in her ear. "In position. Drone's up."

"Lovely. How's the view?"

"One guard, ground floor, desk. One walking the floors on a twenty-minute loop. He's on four now." A pause, which was Victoria watching. "Vitals are live."

They'd been live for a while, technically, but Victoria liked saying it. Evelyn could feel her own pulse picking up, not much, the pleasant tightening that always started when the door got close. Without the regulator nothing evened it out, and nobody had ever made a better drug.

"Going," she said.

---

Side entrance, no camera, because the camera on the side entrance was pointed very confidently at a wall. The stairwell door was where Victoria said, the lock was the model Victoria said, and the little device went onto the panel with a click Evelyn felt more than heard.

Red. She kept her hand off the handle. Red. Green.

"Through."

"Copy. Patrol is on four, moving to three."

Stairs. Her own footsteps, quiet. The floor plan sat in the corner of her right eye, and she didn't need it: up two flights, left door, third desk from the window. The building smelled like carpet cleaner and old coffee. Second landing. Hand on the rail.

"Hold."

She stopped.

"He's taking the stairs," Victoria said. "Not the elevator. He's early."

Above her, one flight up, a door opened. Light spilled down the shaft and steps started coming, slow, the steps of a man thirty-one minutes into an eight-hour shift. Evelyn flattened into the corner under the landing where the rail threw a shadow and stopped breathing loudly.

Three steps. Four. He stopped on the landing directly above her. She could hear him scratching something, maybe his neck.

"Doorbell, hold," Victoria said in her ear, very low. "He's turning."

Evelyn held.

She held perfectly. She'd like that on the record. The guard finished scratching, said something under his breath about the coffee machine, and went back up the way he'd come. The door shut. The light went with it. She stayed in the corner and counted, because Victoria hadn't called it yet, and she did not think about anything at all except the door above her and the weight of her own feet.

"Clear," Victoria said. "He's on four. You have eleven minutes. Go."

"Going."

Third floor, left door, third desk from the window. The desk drawer had a lock a child could have opened, and Evelyn was not a child, so it took her four seconds. The drive was there. She pocketed it and closed the drawer and put the pen back exactly where it had been, because Victoria would ask.

"Package."

"Copy. Exit is the loading door, not the side. Route's in your eye."

Down. Out. The loading door opened onto an alley that smelled like the restaurant next door, and she walked the length of it like someone who'd just finished a late shift, hands in her jacket, and turned left onto the street. Two blocks. The car.

She got in the passenger side. She sat.

She did not say anything. She was a professional.

---

Victoria arrived nine minutes later with the rifle case, the drone case, and the slightly greyer face she always had after a few hours of staring through two displays at once. She loaded the trunk. She got in behind the wheel. She put the drive in the inside pocket where she kept job things and zipped it shut.

"Clean," she said.

"Clean," Evelyn agreed.

Victoria started the car.

Evelyn made it to the first red light, which she thought was very good of her.

"So."

"No," Victoria said.

"I didn't say anything."

"You said 'so.'"

"'So' isn't anything. 'So' is a sound. People make sounds." Evelyn turned sideways in her seat, knee up, to look at her properly. "So you called me Doorbell."

The light turned green. Victoria drove.

"On comms," Evelyn said. "On a job. In the stairwell. Doorbell, hold. Like it was nothing. Like it was the old—like it was just a normal thing that comes out of your mouth."

"It was a hold call."

"It was a *Doorbell* hold call."

"It's shorter," Victoria said.

Evelyn stared at her.

"Two syllables," Victoria said. "Your name is three. Under time pressure, at low volume, the shorter word carries."

"By *one syllable.*"

"One syllable at the right moment is a meaningful saving."

"You've used my whole name on a hundred jobs. More. You've used my whole name while I was hanging off a balcony."

"That was different."

"How?"

"You weren't going anywhere," Victoria said. "You were hanging off a balcony."

Evelyn laughed so hard she had to put her forehead on the window. When she came up for air the city was going by in long soft streaks and Victoria was looking straight ahead, both hands on the wheel, with the particular stillness around her mouth that meant she knew exactly how funny that had been and was not going to participate.

"I'm getting noodles," Evelyn announced. "We're getting noodles. You're coming in. I'm buying."

"You're not buying. You bought that jacket."

"I'm buying with *job money*, Victor, the job we just did, cleanly, as a team, Needle and Doorbell, back together—"

"Evelyn."

"Three syllables. Interesting choice."

---

The noodle place was the one by the overpass that stayed open until four and never asked questions about why people came in at two with grease on their hands. Evelyn ordered the big bowl and a beer and then a second beer, because there was a lot to discuss.

"I just want to understand the decision-making process," she said.

"There was no decision."

"So it was instinct."

"It was a slip," Victoria said, cutting her vegetables into smaller pieces than any vegetable needed to be. "I was watching the guard and the drone feed and your vitals at the same time and I used the old call. It was a habit. It doesn't mean anything."

"A habit from four hundred years ago."

"Five years."

"Five years," Evelyn said, "of you *not* doing it. That's not a habit, that's a—what's a habit that comes back? A relapse."

"I'm not discussing this as a relapse."

"A haunting."

Victoria ate a very small piece of vegetable.

Evelyn had a count, she wasn't going to admit it, but she had one. Victoria had said it out loud maybe four times since they'd left. Once when Jax had needed both of them to confirm their old service numbers for a firmware thing and Victoria had read the whole record off flat, callsign included. Once during a sparring match, as a feint, which had worked, which was rude. Once when—

Anyway. Four.

"Your heart rate went up eleven beats," Victoria said.

Evelyn put her chopsticks down.

"On the landing," Victoria said. "After the call. Eleven beats, for about forty seconds."

"That was the guard."

"The guard was before. You were steady with the guard." Victoria finally looked up. Her expression was perfectly neutral, which on Victoria was a kind of smirk. "It went up after."

"That's—" Evelyn picked her chopsticks back up. "That's a violation. That's private medical data."

"You agreed to live vitals."

"For *safety*. Not for—" She pointed with the chopsticks. "You don't get to watch my heart do things and then *bring it up at dinner.*"

"You're bringing up my comms discipline at dinner."

"Your comms discipline is a matter of public record! It was on the channel!"

"It's a private channel."

"*Our* public record."

The man two stools down, who had been asleep against the counter, woke up, looked at them, and went back to sleep. Victoria took a sip of tea. Evelyn finished her second beer and decided that she was winning.

---

Home. Fifth floor. The elevator worked, which felt like a sign.

Victoria did what she always did: rifle out, cleaned, back in the case; drone checked and charging; the drive logged and locked in the cabinet until Dell's courier came for it; payment confirmed; jacket on its hook. Evelyn lay upside down on the couch with her boots on the armrest and watched her do it, narrating occasionally, and Victoria let her.

When Victoria sat down at the desk to write up the job log, Evelyn rolled off the couch and came to lean on the back of her chair.

"Can I see it?"

"No."

"Is it in there?"

Victoria kept typing. Evelyn leaned further. Victoria tilted the screen, after a moment, the way she did when it would be faster to show her than to have this conversation for another hour.

*22:41. Patrol off schedule, used stairs. Hold called. Non-standard designation used on comms. Noted.*

"Non-standard," Evelyn read.

"It isn't the standard."

"It's a *classic*."

"It's noted," Victoria said, and closed the log.

Evelyn went to bed eventually. Or to her room, which had a bed in it, under some things. She left the door open a crack, the way she usually did, and lay there with her pulse still running a little high from all of it, the job and the beer and the stairwell, and she was grinning at the ceiling like an idiot, and she didn't care.

"Night, Needle," she called.

There was a pause from the living room. Long enough that she thought it wasn't coming.

"Goodnight, Evelyn."

All three syllables. Evelyn laughed into her pillow.

---

Nine days later, on a different door, in a different stairwell, with a different guard who took the stairs, Victoria's voice came through low and even in her ear.

"Evelyn," she said, and gave every syllable its full weight. "Hold."
