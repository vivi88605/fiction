Victoria knew she was sick before she opened her eyes.

There was a particular kind of heaviness that sleep did not produce. It sat behind her eyes and in the joints of her fingers, spread dull and deep through her shoulders, down her spine, into her knees. Her throat hurt when she swallowed. Her skin was too hot beneath the blanket and too cold anywhere the air touched it.

She lay still for several seconds, taking inventory.

Headache. Congestion. Muscle aches. Fever, almost certainly.

Nothing alarming.

Unpleasant, but ordinary.

Victoria opened her eyes.

The clock said 06:17.

She stared at it until the numbers stopped doubling at the edges, then reached automatically for the tablet on the nightstand.

No jobs requiring immediate attention. Two messages from Dell, neither urgent. One system notice she could ignore until tomorrow.

She put the tablet down.

Training could wait.

That decision bothered her enough to confirm she was genuinely ill.

Victoria sat up carefully.

The room shifted sideways.

Not dramatically. Just enough that she stopped moving and waited for it to settle.

Dehydrated from sleeping, perhaps.

She checked her temperature.

High enough to explain most of what she was feeling.

Victoria exhaled through her nose, regretted it when congestion made the attempt mostly pointless, and opened the apartment comm.

Evelyn did not answer.

Of course she didn't. At six in the morning Evelyn was either asleep or had only recently become asleep.

Victoria typed instead.

> I'm sick. Probably contagious. Don't come into my room unless necessary.

She stared at the message.

Added:

> I am fine. I have cancelled work.

Then, after another moment:

> Please wash your hands.

She sent it.

That covered the important points.

Victoria got out of bed.

The floor was cold.

She made it three steps before deciding that her usual morning routine was not merely inadvisable but idiotic.

No training.

No errands.

No checking the apartment systems beyond anything that actively reported a fault.

Tea, water, something to eat, then back to bed.

Reasonable.

Responsible.

She could do reasonable and responsible.

Victoria was awake but had not moved in some time.

She had tried reading.

The book was still open in her lap.

She had read the same paragraph four times and retained none of it.

Victoria looked up.

"No, but apparently if I leave you unsupervised you'll drink three molecules of water and call it hydration."

Evelyn's eyes moved deliberately toward the glass on the nightstand.

It had been half full for several hours.

"Sure."

"Don't."

"Thank you. Put it there."

"Rude. I have a printer."

"My estate is complicated."

That was a mistake.

"There she is."

"Go away."

Victoria sat straighter.

Silence.

Then Evelyn shouted from the kitchen, "I swallowed wrong!"

She remained in her room.

If she needed water, she went to the kitchen when Evelyn was somewhere else.

If Evelyn attempted to bring her something, Victoria accepted only if it prevented unnecessary argument.

The arrangement was efficient.

It was also becoming increasingly difficult.

At four, Victoria stood and had to catch the edge of the desk because the room dimmed.

She stayed there until her vision cleared.

Then she continued.

The kitchen was not far.

It felt farther than usual.

She filled her glass, drank half of it immediately, refilled it, and leaned against the counter while she waited for her pulse to slow.

Not hurt exactly.

Closed.

"Okay," Evelyn said.

Victoria rubbed one hand over her face.

"That wasn't—"

"No, no, you're right. Serious illness. Serious faces only."

"Evelyn."

"What?"

"You're still doing it."

"Doing what?"

Victoria's patience snapped.

"Would you stop hovering?"

Silence.

Evelyn stared at her for a moment.

Then nodded once.

"Sure."

She left.

A door closed.

The apartment went quiet.

Within thirty seconds, Victoria regretted it.

Within a minute, she was too tired to fix it.
