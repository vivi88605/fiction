# 0_Attraction

Lookup-only detail on the romantic feelings between Evelyn and Victoria: how each one's attraction started, peaked, and faded, and how it tied into the face they share. The main character file's "platonic, found-family" dynamic is where this ends up. This file is the history behind it.

# Core

- Both were attracted to each other, at different times. Each knew her own feelings, and Victoria likely knew Evelyn's had once been mutual.
- Neither acted on it. What they already had mattered too much to risk changing. Both could have made a move, so not making one was a choice, not uncertainty.
- The romantic feelings faded, mostly over years of living together. The closeness didn't.
- The attraction was temporary. Staying in each other's lives wasn't.
- Tone to avoid: they didn't fail to notice their feelings, and they don't spend decades yearning over a missed chance. They saw a possibility, turned it down, and found the bond outlasted it. No confession, no slow-burn tension, no regret.

# Timing

- Evelyn falls first and gets over it sooner. Victoria's starts later and lasts longer.
- The service-era stages, year by year (ages 18–22), sit in the by-age table of the service period's arc summary. After service:

| Early merc years | Gone; she's more relaxed and affectionate | Lingers, then slowly fades               |

# Evelyn

## Start

- When she first notices Victoria, Victoria's hair is short, and Evelyn privately thinks she looks handsome. This happens **before** she recognizes her own sold scan in Victoria's face.
- The recognition complicates the attraction instead of causing it. Guilt and morbid fascination come in alongside it (see the main file), and so does a streak of narcissism. She finds the discovery funny, oddly flattering, and impossible to leave alone.
- She can look at her own face in a mirror any time. Getting an expression out of Victoria is more interesting, and Victoria's refusal to play along makes her harder to ignore.
- The early teasing is real attraction, fascination with the face, and the fun of provoking someone who won't indulge her, all at once.

## Crush

- She knows what attraction feels like and has no trouble naming it as a crush. She finds it funny: _of course_ she'd fall for a woman who partly wears her face and finds her infuriating.
- It doesn't need to go anywhere. She lets it exist.
- Over time it shifts from the face to Victoria herself: her personality, her habits, her trust, her company. The resemblance becomes incidental, though Evelyn never stops finding it amusing.

## After

- The crush fades. Her affection and commitment don't.
- She still seeks Victoria out, teases her, crowds her space, tells her ridiculous stories. If anything she gets more casually affectionate once there's no romantic anticipation in it.
- She never doubted she'd stay, so she probably never realizes how much it meant to Victoria that she did.

# Victoria

## Start

- After the reconstruction she has trouble looking at her own reflection. The face doesn't feel like hers.
- Through Evelyn she sees those features every day on someone else, alive and expressive: grinning, concentrating, teasing, sitting next to her. Eventually she already knows what the face she's been avoiding looks like, and avoiding it stops feeling necessary.
- She finds Evelyn attractive **before** she's comfortable with the same features in her own reflection. Slowly the appreciation carries over, and she starts seeing something good in the face she couldn't look at. It's quiet habituation plus affection, and a sense of owning her appearance again.

## Crush

- She notices her attraction clearly and judges it **circumstantial**: constant proximity, shared danger, professional trust, the resemblance, emotional intimacy. She's probably right about the causes, but knowing why doesn't switch the feeling off.
- Evelyn lets the feeling be. Victoria gets stuck assessing it: What would acting on it change? Would it compromise the partnership? Is it stable, or just a response to their situation? Is what they have worth the risk?
- She stays caught in it longer than Evelyn does.

## Evelyn Moving On

- She notices when Evelyn stops looking at her _quite_ the same way and feels a small pang. She understands it's a natural change, not a rejection.
- She had privately wondered how much of Evelyn's attention came from the attraction. She never doubted Evelyn cared, but she couldn't tell which parts of how they were together might change once the crush ended.
- Then nothing important changes. Evelyn still comes looking for her, and the warmth is if anything more relaxed.
- She feels a brief disappointment that Evelyn has moved on romantically and a much stronger **relief that Evelyn hasn't moved away from her**. This is the emotional turning point: she has understood in theory that affection can outlast attraction, and now she's seen it happen.

# Years Later

- Both have mostly stopped feeling romantic attraction. No conversation settled it. The feelings changed with time.
- Their life together is ordinary and woven in: groceries, routines, exhaustion, worrying about each other's safety, annoying domestic habits, quiet evenings.
- Victoria stops trying to decide whether the closeness is romantic or platonic in any permanent sense. She knows what it is in practice, because she's watched it survive the crushes fading.
- For her, the missed romance is a fond, slightly bittersweet memory, not a regret. Turning it down didn't cost her the relationship.
- They could have been lovers. They became something else, and it isn't a consolation prize.
