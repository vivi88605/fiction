# 2_Present_Jobs

## Summary

- They work as a two-person crew: Evelyn is inside, Victoria is outside. Evelyn covers entry, close pressure, and clearing; Victoria covers overwatch, comms, navigation, and the big picture. Victoria's tech side is hardware and firmware, not hacking: live remote hacking happens only when Carol is looped in for the job. Without her, they beat security with physical access and devices prepared in advance. Jax is the medic after the fact.
- Nobody covers Evelyn's back, and Victoria carries too much at once. Most of their methods exist to patch those two gaps.
- Work-day shape: Dell oversells a job -> Carol supplies intel/Victoria verifies -> approach (Victoria tenses up, Evelyn gets keyed up) -> old-unit coordination during -> aftermath differs if clean (food/drinks/reset) vs bad (Victoria quiet, Evelyn funnier/darker) -> Jax if anyone's hurt.
- They take jobs below megacorp level, where security is automated but dumb. They walk away from megacorp targets, military tech, and anything Victoria can't verify.
- Each also takes smaller, low-risk solo work — Evelyn close-range and physical, Victoria distance, surveillance, and hardware — while the other watches their vitals.
- They stay alive by always having a way out and by aborting early. Intensity usually comes from routine jobs that go wrong rather than from jobs that were dangerous by design.

For the phase-by-phase breakdown, role split, and job types, read the rest of this file.

## Two-Person Crew

### Who covers what

- A full squad would split these roles; the two of them absorb them.
  - **Point / entry:** Evelyn.
  - **Clearing, rear security:** Evelyn, badly — no one is watching her back.
  - **Overwatch / precision fire:** Victoria.
  - **Comms, navigation, overall picture:** Victoria.
  - **Tech:** split three ways. Evelyn does hands-on bypasses at the door. Victoria does hardware and firmware: reading which models and versions are weak, building and maintaining devices, physical tricks on panels and lines. Live remote hacking (cameras, network, alarms) is Carol's alone, and only if she's looped in.
  - **Intel:** Carol, remotely, before the job and live during it when she's looped in.
  - **Medic:** nobody during the job. Jax afterward.
- The imbalance: Victoria is overloaded, and Evelyn is blind behind her.
- Looping Carol in adds live hacking they otherwise don't have. It costs part of the pay, and Carol only sees what the network shows her, so on bigger or riskier jobs they usually pay for it and on small ones they usually don't.

### Being far away

- Victoria's position is usually a rooftop or window a few hundred meters out, so reaching Evelyn physically takes minutes.
- Before closing the distance she works from where she is: shooting through a window, sending the drone, triggering a device planted earlier, talking Evelyn through a route only she can see. With Carol on the line, cameras can be looped and doors locked remotely too.
- Leaving her position is a last resort and costs her cover, so she rarely does it.

## Work Day

### Before the job

- Dell sends the contract and presents it in the most favorable plausible terms.
- Victoria reads everything carefully and asks inconvenient questions.
- Evelyn focuses on breach points, risk, pay, and whether something can be blown open unnecessarily.
- Carol supplies verified remote intel.
- Victoria cross-checks key details rather than relying on a single source.
- Victoria briefs Evelyn on door materials, lock hardware, and new security builds — the reading side of breaching that Evelyn neglects.
- Victoria picks her position, Evelyn's exit route, and at least one fallback for each.
- Without Carol on the job, Victoria prepares the devices: which cameras get looped, which panels get swapped, and where each one gets planted.
- Before bigger jobs, they sometimes run room-entry drills out of old-unit habit. Evelyn takes entry and Victoria takes the overwatch role she'll have on the actual job.
- On long prep nights, Victoria switches from tea to coffee.
- Gear gets inspected before departure.

### Approach

- Victoria grows quieter and starts building contingencies, already assuming at least one thing will go wrong.
- Evelyn becomes visibly more energized as danger gets closer. With her adrenal regulator disabled, nothing blunts the spike — and she likes it that way.
- Live vitals get switched on. They monitor each other's location and vitals without discussing it.
- Victoria can see Evelyn's unregulated adrenaline climbing on the vitals feed. She doesn't comment on it, but she doesn't stop watching it either.

### During the job

- They fall back into old-unit coordination easily.
- Evelyn handles entry, disruption, close pressure, and rapid movement.
- Victoria controls distance, angles, information, and technical problems.
- They rarely need long verbal exchanges once things start moving.
- Victoria notices risk early and tries to stop problems before they become emergencies.
- Evelyn is more willing to accept physical risk, especially when adrenaline is involved. The most reliable brake on it is knowing Victoria will be the one frightened if she gets hurt.
- Phase by phase:
  - **Setup.** Victoria settles in, puts up the drone, and watches the approach. Evelyn plants sensors, jammers, and any prepared devices on the way in.
  - **Entry.** Victoria calls the timing while Evelyn works the door. The cameras are blinded by Carol remotely, or by a device planted earlier. Evelyn is most exposed here: hands busy, head down, relying completely on Victoria.
  - **Inside.** Victoria loses most of her sightlines and switches from shooting to supplying information: floor plans, thermal, camera feeds from a planted tap or from Carol, movement warnings. If Carol is on the job, she's opening doors and holding off alarms in the background at the same time. When Evelyn goes silent, Victoria reads the vitals feed.
  - **Objective.** Victoria watches the outside — reinforcements, vehicles, response times — and calls the time limit.
  - **Exit.** Victoria goes back to shooting, covering Evelyn's route and dealing with pursuit. Evelyn leaves by the route Victoria picked, which may not be the way she came in.
  - **Victoria's exit.** Victoria breaks down her position and gets out alone while Evelyn is somewhere else. If the area is locked down, her weakness at close range becomes the problem.

### After the job

- **When it's clean**
  - Evelyn wants food, alcohol, noise, or some combination of all three.
  - Victoria wants payment confirmed, injuries checked, gear cleaned, and everything put back in order.
  - Evelyn downplays minor injuries — Victoria can usually tell something is off from Evelyn's gait or how she holds herself, before Evelyn says a word.
- **When it's bad**
  - Victoria gets quieter.
  - Evelyn gets funnier.
  - The darker the situation, the harder Evelyn leans on jokes and deflection. Victoria usually reads this correctly rather than assuming she simply does not care.
- **If someone is injured**
  - Jax becomes the next stop.
  - Evelyn insists she can walk in herself if physically possible.
  - Jax and Evelyn immediately start making bad jokes.
  - Victoria meets the worst of them with a flat glare and claims she cannot stand either of them — she stays regardless, and enjoys it more than she would ever admit.

### Back home

- Victoria resets gear and the apartment.
- Evelyn either crashes, or keeps moving long past when she should.
- The next day often looks surprisingly ordinary again.

## Where It Breaks

- **Victoria overloaded.** Too many things need her at once; she drops one, and Evelyn walks into it.
- **Unexpected networked security.** A job planned without Carol runs into a system nobody identified in prep, and neither of them can hack it.
- **Carol cut off.** The network goes down, the security isn't networked at all, or Carol's link drops. The live hacking is gone mid-job, and they fall back on hardware tricks and physical access.
- **Evelyn out of sight.** A basement, a shielded room, a jammed floor. Victoria has only the vitals feed, or nothing.
- **Victoria's position found.** Evelyn is deep inside and can't help.
- **Evelyn pinned with no rear security.** Something comes from a direction she assumed was clear.

## Jobs They Take

### Routine (the income)

- **Retrieval from small operators.** A drive from a shell company's office, a stolen item back from a pawn broker. Cameras, a lock, maybe one guard.
- **Escort or overwatch for a deal.** Victoria on a roof, Evelyn close to the client. Usually nothing happens.
- **Repossession.** Vehicles, hardware, or financed cyberware for lenders who'd rather not go to court. Grimy and morally grey.
- **Security testing.** A small business pays them to break in and prove its security doesn't work.
- **Planting or pulling a device.** A network tap for a client, or a bug that needs to come back out.

### Harder (the well-paying contract)

- **Extraction.** Getting a person out — a defecting mid-level employee, someone's kid from a gang, a witness. The package is slow, scared, and doesn't follow plans.
- **Sabotage.** A server room, chop shop, or warehouse belonging to a gang or small corp. Evelyn gets to blow something open.
- **Clearing out a crew.** A landlord or small business wants squatters or a rival gone. Turns close-range fast.
- **Counter-sniping.** Someone has a price on their head; the job is finding the shooter first. Victoria's field, with their usual roles reversed.

### Turned down, or walked away from

- Anything directly against a megacorp's own security: corporate armies, AI-run systems, a response they can't outrun.
- Military-owned tech on site — smart-linked guns or optical camo mean military or corporate-army involvement, and they leave.
- Assassinations of anyone important; too much heat afterward.
- Jobs Victoria can't verify.

## Solo Jobs

- Smaller, lower-risk work either of them takes alone, usually through Dell, to fill a slow week or when the other is injured, recovering, or busy.
- The other one watch the solo one's location and vitals from home, or from a car nearby. Live vitals get switched on just like on a joint job.
- Solo work stays deliberately low-risk, because each of them alone is missing half of what keeps them alive.

### Evelyn

- **Hired breacher for another crew.** Another crew needs a door opened. She makes the entry and goes in first, then leaves the rest to them. It pays well for a short job, but she's trusting strangers to cover her, and Victoria dislikes it more than anything else Evelyn does alone.
- **Door and security work at a venue.** A club or event needs someone quick and intimidating at the door. It's low-stakes and noisy, and she gets paid to be around people.
- **Vehicle repossession.** Get in, start it, drive it away. It's fast and physical, and it counts as fun.
- **Close protection for a short stint.** Walking a nervous client through one meeting or one night out. Strictly close range, with no expectation of a sniper on either side.
- **Lockout and salvage jobs.** Getting into a sealed unit, a seized storage space, or a dead business's back room for whoever owns it now.
- **What she's missing:** eyes outside and anyone watching her back. On her own she's a lot more careful than her reputation says, or she's supposed to be.

### Victoria

- **Hired overwatch for another crew.** She takes a roof for someone else's job. She insists on checking the job herself first, and she's there for the rifle, not to be part of their team.
- **Surveillance and stakeouts.** Watching a building, a person, or a schedule for days, and logging patterns for a client, Carol, or their own future job.
- **Counter-surveillance sweeps.** Finding bugs, taps, and tampered hardware in someone's home, office, or car. This is pure hardware work.
- **Hardware and firmware work.** Repairing or reflashing security gear for small businesses, building devices for Carol, and fixing salvaged electronics for resale. It's quiet, reliable income.
- **Verifying intel.** Going out in person to confirm what a client or broker claims: guard counts, camera models, entry points.
- **What she's missing:** anyone at close range. She keeps her solo work at a distance, or low-profile enough that nobody should come looking for her. The drills with Evelyn are for when that fails.

## Why They're Still Alive

- Every job has a way out, and Victoria has a fallback for it.
- They abort early. Their reputation is reliable, not reckless, so walking away costs a payment rather than their standing.
- Most jobs are routine. Intense ones are usually routine jobs gone wrong — rather than jobs that were dangerous by design.
