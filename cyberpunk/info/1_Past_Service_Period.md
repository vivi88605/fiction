# 1_Past_Service_Period

## Arc Summary

- How they met: Victoria is catastrophically injured (loses both eyes/upper half of her face) in a different squad, reassigned to Turquoise Nine as its new sniper/technician. Evelyn (then in Cateye Seven) recognizes her own previously sold biometric data in Victoria's reconstructed face and becomes fixated.
- Evelyn engineers her own transfer into Turquoise Nine (making herself Victoria's subordinate) despite Victoria's open hostility.
- Relationship arc: guilt/curiosity -> grudging professional trust built on missions -> Evelyn goes off-plan to rescue Victoria under fire -> Victoria realizes Evelyn matters when she panics over a comms blackout -> "we are not friends" bit -> settled, acknowledged friendship by the end of their service.
- Ends with both choosing not to renew, walking away together into the mercenary life shown in the present-day file.

The rest of this file covers the setting: Concord, its culture and its wars; the CEF and its squads; and the training and service timeline.

## Concord Group

- Megacorp; broad conglomerate spanning defense, cyberware, and infrastructure, with its own military arm (the CEF).

### Culture & Control

- Ideological state-hybrid culture — service is framed as duty and purification, not just employment; recruits are told they're the stabilizing force holding a fractured world together.
- Framed publicly as "duty of care" — the corp insists it tracks every soldier's vitals and position because it values them, because no one gets left behind. In practice, it's total control: real-time behavioral data, loyalty monitoring, no meaningful privacy on or off duty.
- Rhetoric says every soldier matters to the mission; budget says otherwise — resources are quietly allocated by internal priority, not by the stated ideology, and nobody frames this as hypocrisy internally.
- Intelligence tends to get filtered upstream to fit the "we are winning / we are righteous" narrative rather than reflect raw ground truth.

### Stabilization Contracts

- Concord's military business is selling "stabilization": governments and other corps contract it to secure a region — its security, infrastructure, and resource sites. The CEF is how Concord delivers.
- The enemy is whoever is local and armed: separatists, militias, rival corps' deniable proxies.
- Contracts pay out on milestones. A sector marked _stabilized_ is a billable event. Nobody is told to shade the intel; the incentive just runs through every layer of consolidation, so reports round off toward "winning" one defensible step at a time.

## Concord Expeditionary Force (CEF)

- The military arm of Concord Group.
- Line squads are named gem + number (Turquoise Nine, Cateye Seven, Garnet Four, …). The convention is routine across the force, not a marker of status. Squads are often shortened to the gem alone (e.g. "Turquoise").
- Above the squad sits a thin layer: Sector Command assigns squads to sectors and consolidates intel into squad-level briefs. Squads rarely deal with anyone higher.
- Callsigns: the squad leader is always "Actual." Everyone else carries a personal callsign that stays with them from squad to squad.

## Training (age 15–18)

Academy track rather than boot camp — replaces normal schooling.

- Training sections are named by gem-cutting stage, not by gem: _Rough_ + number for the general academy years, _Facet_ + number for the specialty year. A recruit only gets a gem-numbered squad on assignment. In the corp's framing, nobody gets a gem until they're finished being cut.
- Evaluations borrow gem-grading language: scores are _clarity_ and _cut_; a disciplinary mark is an _inclusion_ on your record.

### Enrollment (age 15)

- The academy costs nothing up front — room, board, schooling — with the bill deferred into the service debt. It draws two kinds of recruits: kids with nothing better on offer, and kids raised inside Concord's orbit for whom it's the natural next step.
- Recruits declare an intended specialty at intake.
- Sections are squad-shaped: each holds every role in roughly squad proportions, so a section of about 24 has three or four would-be breachers, a few marksman/techs, gunners, medics, and so on.
- A recruit's section is who they live, condition, and run exercises with, which is where they learn to work across roles. Specialty classes pull the same track together from several sections, so recruits know their own year's track-mates by sight even across sections.

### General Academy (age 15–17, _Rough_)

- Two years: education, physical conditioning, ideological indoctrination.
- Residential sections share dorms, mess, and schedule, all monitored. The section becomes a recruit's whole social world.
- Mostly shared curriculum: schooling in the mornings (maths, languages, and Concord's version of history and civics, taught as "Duty"), conditioning in the afternoons, and basic soldiering for everyone — marksmanship, first aid, comms, squad movement. Intro classes for each recruit's intended specialty run alongside.
- Aptitude is tested continuously. A recruit can change specialty any time before the Facet year; they switch classes and stay in their section.

### Specialty Year (age 17–18, _Facet_)

- The section carries its number and people into the specialty year (Rough Four → Facet Four).
- Role-specific certification in track classes, alongside section-level exercises where the roles work together.
- Standard package (spine/neck and core bone reinforcement (limbs left natural), comm implant, vitals monitor, adrenal regulator) installed early in this year, alongside role-specific implants.
- Rehab and recalibration aren't scheduled separately — the corp folds them into the rest of the year's conditioning. What reads as continued physical training is partly recruits relearning their own bodies: movement with a reinforced skeleton, reaction time with a live booster. Concord doesn't build in recovery time so much as absorb it into the curriculum — bodies brought online, not patients cared for.
- Full calibration isn't guaranteed by graduation.
- The year ends with final evaluations, then **Setting**: assignment day, when each recruit is posted to a gem-numbered squad — the gem set into its mount. A section scatters across the force at Setting — by design. The academy builds squad-shaped sections, lets them bond, and then breaks them up: a recruit's loyalty belongs to Concord, not to their section, and any part should fit any mount.

## Active Service (age 18+)

- Squads rotate: roughly 3–4 months at forward staging areas, then a few weeks at a rear base for refit, implant maintenance, retraining, and replacements. Squads from different sectors share staging areas and bases, which puts strangers from different squads side by side.
- The work: breaching raids, overwatch, securing Concord infrastructure and sites, convoy escort, and the sweeps that come before a sector is marked _stabilized_. Some of it is plain garrison duty where nothing happens.
- Every soldier can check their contribution ledger, which counts down the months until their debt clears. Anything added along the way, like a replacement implant, goes onto it.
- Service in CEF has a defined "contribution period" rather than being indefinite — completing it, or being medically discharged, is treated as honorable.
- The academy training is nominally free — but tuition, along with the cost of the implants installed during the specialty year, is clawed back if a recruit leaves before serving a minimum term. Four years of active service is the point where that debt clears.
- Evelyn and Victoria served exactly that minimum, then chose not to renew — not a medical discharge, not desertion, just staying long enough to walk away clean, financially as well as contractually.
