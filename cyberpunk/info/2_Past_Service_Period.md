# 3_Past_Service_Period

## Hierarchy

- **Concord Group (Megacorp)**
  - Broad conglomerate with a military arm, spanning defense, cyberware, and infrastructure.
  - Ideological state-hybrid culture — service is framed as duty and purification, not just employment; recruits are told they're the stabilizing force holding a fractured world together.
  - Framed publicly as "duty of care" — the corp insists it tracks every soldier's vitals and position because it values them, because no one gets left behind. In practice, it's total control: real-time behavioral data, loyalty monitoring, no meaningful privacy on or off duty.
  - Rhetoric says every soldier matters to the mission; budget says otherwise — resources are quietly allocated by internal priority, not by the stated ideology, and nobody frames this as hypocrisy internally.
  - Intelligence tends to get filtered upstream to fit the "we are winning / we are righteous" narrative rather than reflect raw ground truth.

- **Concord Expeditionary Force (Army)**
  - The military arm of Concord Group.
  - Abbreviated as CEF.

- **Turquoise Nine (Squad)**
  - An ordinary line squad — one of many gem-numbered squads in the CEF.
  - The naming convention is routine across the force, not a marker of status.
  - Abbreviated as Turquoise.
  - **Roster**
    - Field Officer (squad leader)
    - Senior Operative (second-in-command)
    - **Evelyn** — Operative, Rated Breacher
    - **Victoria** — Senior Operative, Rated Marksman/Technician
    - Rifleman / Support Gunner ×2
    - Combat Medic

  > - Victoria outranks Evelyn slightly (Senior Operative vs. Operative).

## Rough Timeline

- **Training**: age 15–18, academy track rather than boot camp — replaces normal schooling.
  - Two years general academy: education, physical conditioning, ideological indoctrination.
  - One year specialty track: role-specific certification.
- **Active service**: age 18–22, four years in CEF.

> - Service in the CEF has a defined "contribution period" rather than being indefinite — completing it, or being medically discharged, is treated as honorable.
> - The academy training is nominally free — but tuition is clawed back if a recruit leaves before serving a minimum term. Four years is the point where that debt clears.
> - Evelyn and Victoria served exactly that minimum, then chose not to renew — not a medical discharge, not desertion, just staying long enough to walk away clean, financially as well as contractually.

## Evelyn and Victoria Meet

### Premise

- Victoria is injured while serving in some earlier squad. A position she was covering got hit based on intel that turned out to be wrong. The blast cost her both eyes and half her face; skull reinforcement was the only reason it wasn't fatal.
- Months of surgery, reconstruction, rehab, and retraining follow.
- Her former billet is filled in the meantime.
- Once medically cleared, she gets reassigned to Turquoise Nine as their Senior Operative / marksman-technician.
- Evelyn is still with Cateye Seven.
- Evelyn sees Victoria, stares, and the whole thing starts.

### “Why are you staring at me?”

- Cateye Seven and Turquoise Nine are temporarily operating out of the same staging area / base.
- Evelyn spots Victoria and immediately gets that uncanny _wait, what the fuck?_ feeling.
- Victoria does **not** recognize the resemblance at first because she has been avoiding mirrors since her reconstruction.
- Evelyn stares too long.
- Victoria notices and assumes Evelyn is being rude, mocking her injuries, or just generally being weird.
- Evelyn tries to explain and makes it worse.
- Something like: “This is going to sound bad,”
- Victoria: “Then don’t say it.”
- Victoria is being confronted with the face she has deliberately refused to look at.

### The realization

- Evelyn gets Victoria to a reflective glass and actually compare them somehow.
- Victoria finally sees it.
- Immediate silence.
- Evelyn is fascinated.
- Victoria is horrified and furious.
- The resemblance is not exact enough that strangers instantly mistake them for each other, but once pointed out it is unmistakable.
- Evelyn, attempting to lighten the mood: “On the bright side, excellent taste.”
- Victoria: “Leave me alone.”
- Evelyn does not leave her alone.

### Victoria verifies everything anyway

- Victoria already believes Evelyn because she can see the resemblance. But she checks the records regardless. She traces the biometric sale, contractor, reconstruction package, dates, authorization chain. She just refuses to trust information she has not verified herself.
- Evelyn catches her doing it.
- “You know we have the same nose, right?”
- “Similarity is not provenance.”
- “I’m putting that on a shirt.”
- Victoria discovers exactly how bureaucratically mundane the whole thing was. She simply received the cheapest acceptable reconstruction.

### Evelyn keeps appearing

- Evelyn becomes intensely curious about Victoria.
  - Some of it is guilt. Some is morbid fascination.
  - Some is just that Victoria is extremely fun to annoy.
- She starts finding increasingly weak reasons to be near Turquoise Nine.
  - Borrows equipment.
  - Uses their mess rotation.
  - “Accidentally” takes the same transit.
  - Volunteers for cross-squad exercises.
- Victoria notices immediately.
- “Are you following me?”
- “Following is such an ugly word.”
- “You have entered our ready room four times today.”
- “I like the lighting.”
- Soldiers are monitored so closely that Victoria can probably prove this with actual logs.

### Victoria dislikes Evelyn on principle

- She finds Evelyn invasive, unserious, reckless, and deeply irritating.
- Worse: Evelyn keeps joking about things Victoria considers serious.
- Evelyn is not actually trying to be cruel; joking is simply her default defense mechanism, especially when she is uncomfortable.
- Victoria mistakes some of that for insensitivity.
- Evelyn mistakes Victoria’s hostility for a challenge.
- Both are technically correct.

### Evelyn hears Turquoise Nine needs a breacher

- A vacancy opens, or their existing breacher rotates out / is reassigned / gets medically discharged.
- Evelyn learns this and immediately starts scheming.
- Not grand espionage. Just relentless military bureaucracy exploitation.
  - She requests transfer.
  - Re-files after rejection.
  - Volunteers for evaluations.
  - Points out that Cateye Seven has redundant capability.
  - Bugs exactly the right administrator until approving it becomes easier than refusing.
- people are interchangeable pieces in the organization, which is exactly why Evelyn can make the move happen.

### Victoria discovers the transfer

- Ideally, Victoria learns after it is already approved.
- “You transferred into my squad.”
- “Apparently.”
- “You requested it.”
- “There was paperwork involved, yes.”
- “Why?”
- Evelyn gives some useless answer like, “Professional development.” Victoria does not believe her for one second.
- Because Victoria slightly outranks Evelyn, this also means Evelyn has deliberately made herself Victoria’s subordinate.
- Victoria considers this insane.
- Evelyn considers it hilarious.

### First missions together — unfortunately, they work

- This is where Victoria’s attitude begins shifting despite herself.
- Their roles fit naturally:
  - Victoria scouts / identifies threats / gives technical information.
  - Evelyn makes the entry and goes through first.
- Their professional dynamic is inherently trust-based.
- Victoria gives Evelyn precise calls, Evelyn follows them instantly.
- No debate, no ego, no second-guessing. Victoria finds this surprising because Evelyn is otherwise allergic to caution.
- Evelyn: “See? I listen.”
- Victoria: “Only under live-fire conditions, apparently.”

### Victoria realizes Evelyn trusts her information

- This matters more to Victoria than Evelyn understands.
- Victoria’s defining trauma happened because the intelligence she relied on was wrong.
- So Evelyn throwing herself through a doorway based purely on Victoria saying _clear left, two right_ is not trivial.
- Evelyn does not make a speech about trust. She just acts like Victoria being right is obvious.
- That starts breaking through Victoria’s hostility.

### The rescue

- Victoria’s position gets compromised.
- Evelyn abandons a cleaner tactical option and goes back for her.
- Victoria protests over comms because it is inefficient / dangerous / not necessary.
- Evelyn: “Yeah, okay.”
- Continues doing it.
- She gets Victoria out.
- Victoria is angry afterward because Evelyn took an unnecessary risk.
- Evelyn is confused because, to her, there was never a decision.
- “You were there.”
- That hits Victoria harder than any dramatic declaration would.
- Concord constantly says nobody gets left behind, while quietly assigning human value by operational priority.
- Evelyn actually means it personally.

### The “we are not friends” phase

- Evelyn has already decided they are friends.
- Victoria strongly disagrees.
- Running bit:
  - Evelyn: “My friend Victoria—”
  - Victoria: “No.”
  - Evelyn: “My hostile acquaintance Victoria—”
- Meanwhile Victoria:
  - checks Evelyn’s gear,
  - notices small injuries,
  - corrects her route planning,
  - saves her food,
  - reminds her about medical appointments,
  - knows exactly when Evelyn is lying by omission.
- Victoria’s affection shows through actions long before words; she is sharp verbally but gentle in practice.
- Evelyn finds the contradiction endlessly entertaining.

### Victoria starts noticing

- Victoria catches inconsistencies.
- Evelyn is cheerful after things that should rattle her; Takes risks that are not quite normal even for a breacher.
- Laughs too quickly after getting hurt; Jokes become more intense when situations get worse.
- Victoria initially interprets this as recklessness.
- Eventually she realizes it is something closer to self-neglect.
- Evelyn’s humor and danger-seeking are both coping mechanisms, and at this point she is in a much darker place internally than she appears.
- Victoria does not confront her directly yet.
- She just starts watching more closely.

### Evelyn goes missing on comms

- During an operation, Evelyn’s signal disappears.
- Thirty seconds.
- Then longer.
- Victoria becomes disproportionately tense.
- Everyone else is still treating it as an operational problem; Victoria has already jumped mentally to _she is dead_.
- That is the moment Victoria realizes Evelyn matters to her, since her own internal marker for attachment is whether she catches herself bracing for someone’s death.
- Evelyn eventually comes back:
  - “Good news.”
  - Victoria: “No.”
  - “You don’t even know what I was going to say.”
  - “Nothing beginning with those words has ever been good.”
- Victoria is furious afterward.
- Evelyn jokes until she notices Victoria’s hands are shaking.
- Then the joke dies halfway out of her mouth.

### Victoria begins choosing Evelyn back

- Up to now, Evelyn has driven almost all personal contact.
- Then Victoria starts initiating small things:
  - sitting beside her at meals,
  - asking for her specifically on a task,
  - bringing her tea/coffee equivalent without comment,
  - waiting for her after debrief,
  - sending her information she knows Evelyn will find interesting.
- Evelyn notices instantly.
- She does **not** mention it because even she understands that would make Victoria stop.
- Possibly the greatest act of restraint Evelyn has ever performed.

### Settled friendship

- Nobody formally declares anything.
- At some point, everyone in Turquoise Nine just assumes they come as a pair.
- Someone refers to Victoria as Evelyn’s friend.
- Victoria opens her mouth to correct them.
- Pauses.
- “...Yes.”
- Evelyn looks delighted.
- Victoria immediately regrets giving her this much emotional ammunition.

### End-state of the arc

- The face is what makes Evelyn notice Victoria.
- Guilt makes her stay nearby.
- Professional trust gives them a reason to rely on each other.
- Repeated danger makes them emotionally important to one another.
- And somewhere along the way, the bizarre coincidence becomes almost irrelevant.
- They stop being **“the woman whose face was used for mine”** and **“the woman walking around with my face.”**
- They just become Evelyn and Victoria.
